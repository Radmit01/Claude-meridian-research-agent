# Research Pack: Where has Protiviti worked with Meridian before, and what did we learn?

## 1. Findings

### Prior Protiviti engagements with Meridian: internal audit co-source (2023 proposal, FY2024 delivery)

- In 2023, Protiviti submitted a proposal to Meridian for internal audit co-source services covering technology, cyber and financial crime audit disciplines. [PV-01, Client Context]
- The proposed scope comprised specialist audit resource across cyber, cloud, data and financial crime; an agreed number of audits per annum drawn from the approved audit plan; methodology alignment to the client's approach; and QA and knowledge transfer to the in-house team. [PV-01, Proposed Scope]
- The commercial model was a blended day rate with an annual minimum commitment, a rate card by grade, and a flexible drawdown mechanism to accommodate changes to the plan during the year. [PV-01, Commercial Model]
- Protiviti presented three differentiators: combined internal audit and specialist technology capability in a single team; regulatory fluency across the UK, Ireland, Luxembourg and Asia; and the ability to flex specialist resource without renegotiation. [PV-01, Differentiators Presented]
- Protiviti was appointed for a two-year term commencing in 2024, covering a subset of the proposed disciplines. The financial crime audit scope was retained in-house at the client's election. [PV-01, Outcome]
- In year one of the two-year co-source term, fourteen audits were delivered against a planned fifteen; one was deferred at the client's request. [PV-05, Engagement Summary]
- The audits covered cloud security configuration (UK and Luxembourg), group-wide identity and access management, vulnerability management, third-party onboarding and monitoring, data protection and cross-border transfers, and change management over phase one of the modernisation programme, plus eight further technology operations and infrastructure audits. [PV-05, Audits Delivered]
- The sanctions screening tuning audit was deferred at the client's request and carried forward. [PV-05, Deferred Audit]

### Prior Protiviti engagements with Meridian: cyber security assessment (2025)

- In 2025, Protiviti submitted a proposal for an assessment of cyber security controls across the Meridian Group estate, focused on cloud configuration and identity and access management. [PV-02, Client Context]
- The scope covered cyber control maturity against a recognised framework; cloud security baselines across the UK, Luxembourg and Singapore; privileged access management and joiners/movers/leavers processes; vulnerability management service levels; and logging and monitoring coverage against the business-critical application inventory. [PV-02, Proposed Scope]
- The proposed team comprised an engagement director, an engagement manager, two cyber specialists and a cloud security specialist, supported by delivery centre resource for evidence analysis. [PV-02, Proposed Team Shape]
- Protiviti was appointed. Fieldwork was conducted during 2025, and reporting was issued to the Technology and Operational Resilience Committee. [PV-02, Outcome]

### What we learned: findings and relationship lessons from delivery

- The reported themes were: inconsistent security baselines across regional entities, with Asian entities materially behind; weak vulnerability remediation, with service levels repeatedly missed; absent third-party exit planning for two of three critical suppliers; fragmented identity and access management, with no single view of privileged access; and, in modernisation programme assurance, optimistic milestone reporting and insufficient independent challenge. [PV-05, Principal Themes Reported]
- The engagement was strongest with the Chief Internal Auditor and the CTO. Access to the second line financial crime team was limited because that scope was retained in-house, and scheduling was disrupted twice by client resource availability. [PV-05, Relationship Observations]

### Adjacent opportunities discussed but not commissioned

- In 2025, Protiviti discussed potential support beyond the existing internal audit co-source arrangement. [PV-09, Background]
- The areas discussed but not commissioned were: independent validation of financial crime remediation closure, which was under consideration but deferred pending programme completion; operational resilience dependency mapping, which was to be delivered internally first; third-party exit planning, which was scoped but not commissioned; and AI governance framework design, which was discussed exploratorily with the CTO. [PV-09, Areas Discussed with the Client]
- The existing rate card applies. Additional scope would be contracted as a separate SOW under the existing master services agreement. [PV-09, Commercial Position]
- The client had not confirmed whether the FY2026 co-source arrangement would be extended, expanded to include financial crime disciplines, or re-tendered. [PV-09, Open Items at Time of Writing]

### Client context relevant to follow-on work (Meridian's own documents; no Protiviti engagement referenced)

- Meridian is a London-headquartered, mid-size financial services group with operations in the UK, Ireland, Luxembourg, Singapore and Hong Kong. It has about 4,800 staff and GBP 91.4bn in assets under administration, and operates three segments: Wealth & Asset Management (46% of revenue), Retail Banking (34%) and Institutional Services (20%). [CL-01, Section 1 — Group Overview]
- The principal risks are financial crime (especially in Institutional Services), operational resilience and third-party concentration, conduct in Retail Banking, data protection in cloud migration, technology change, and AI governance. [CL-01, Section 3 — Principal Risks and Uncertainties]
- The Board was told that the financial crime remediation programme, initiated after the 2024 supervisory review, is 'substantially complete': all twelve high-priority findings have been remediated and closed, with closure evidence accepted by the second line. Three medium-priority findings remain open and are scheduled for closure within the quarter. [CL-05, Item 6 — Financial Crime Remediation Programme]
- The Board recorded its satisfaction and asked that the programme be prepared for formal closure, with the remaining medium-priority items tracked through business-as-usual governance. Closure was accepted by the second line, with no mention of independent validation. [CL-05, Item 6 — Financial Crime Remediation Programme]
- Management states that substantial progress has been made across the financial crime remediation portfolio, but that certain workstreams remain in delivery and will extend into the next financial year. This is more cautious than the 'substantially complete' language in the Q3 board minutes. The conflict is UNRESOLVED: no dates are given, both sources are authored by Meridian, they may cover different scopes (high-priority findings versus the broader portfolio), and neither states that closure was independently validated. [CL-01, Section 5 — Statement on Remediation Activity]
- The Board approved an increase in the onboarding volume appetite threshold in Institutional Services on the basis that financial crime controls had been remediated and were assessed as effective. [CL-05, Item 7 — Risk Appetite Review]
- Important business services have been identified, and impact tolerances are being set. Third-party dependency mapping for the four most critical services remains incomplete. The Board requested a further update. [CL-05, Item 8 — Operational Resilience]
- Meridian depends on three critical technology suppliers, but exit plans exist for only one of them. Audit rights and incident notification provisions are inconsistent across the suppliers. The Board requested a remediation plan by the end of Q1 2026. [CL-06, Item 5 — Third-Party Risk]
- The programme is about four months behind baseline. Wave one of the data platform migration has slipped to Q2 2026, and forecast spend has increased by GBP 14 million. [CL-06, Item 4 — Core Platform Modernisation Programme]
- The FY2026 plan expands coverage of financial crime, operational resilience, third-party management, cloud security and AI governance. Full delivery requires specialist resource beyond the current establishment, and a co-source arrangement was being considered. This is a possible opening for external support, but no provider is named. [CL-06, Item 6 — Internal Audit Plan FY2026]
- The FY2026 internal audit plan comprises 38 audits. It is risk-based and covers financial crime, operational resilience, third-party risk, technology and cyber, change, conduct, data and privacy, and AI governance. [CL-09, Planning Basis]
- Under financial crime, the plan includes closure validation of the remediation programme, as well as sanctions screening effectiveness and tuning, transaction monitoring, CDD and EDD. [CL-09, Thematic Coverage]
- The plan requires about 6,200 audit days against 4,900 days of internal capacity. The shortfall of about 1,300 days is expected to be met through co-source arrangements, mainly in cyber, cloud, financial crime and resilience. No co-source provider is named. [CL-09, Resourcing]
- Four FY2025 audits were deferred into FY2026: sanctions screening tuning, Hong Kong entity governance, model risk management and third-party exit planning. The AI governance readiness review is deferred pending establishment of the framework. [CL-09, Prior Year Carry-Forward]

### Protiviti methodology, perspective and credentials (not Meridian-specific engagement history)

- The credentials are sanitised examples of work for other financial services clients. They are not prior Meridian engagements, though they are relevant to the adjacent areas discussed with Meridian. The extracts do not name Meridian. [PV-03, Credential 1 — Financial Crime Remediation Validation, European Bank]
- For another client, Protiviti tested closure evidence for 41 findings and found that 9 had been closed without sufficient evidence and required reopening. This is a capability comparable to the Meridian validation scope discussed in PV-09. [PV-03, Credential 1 — Financial Crime Remediation Validation, European Bank]
- For a payments institution, Protiviti carried out an independent review of screening configuration, fuzzy matching thresholds and above/below-the-line testing. This is comparable to the sanctions screening tuning audit deferred at Meridian (PV-05). [PV-03, Credential 6 — Sanctions Screening Tuning Review, Payments Institution]
- This is a generic Protiviti methodology extract with five phases. It does not describe any specific engagement with Meridian or lessons learned there. [PV-04, Phase 1 — Planning and Risk Assessment]
- Protiviti treats closure validation as a distinct assurance activity. Closure evidence is tested independently against the original finding, and a finding is closed only where the underlying control is shown to operate effectively. This contrasts with reliance on management assertion. [PV-04, Closure Validation Principle]
- PV-08 is a Protiviti point of view on general patterns and failure modes. These include growth approved on a remediation programme reported as complete without independent validation of closure evidence, governance forums constituted after AI deployment, and resourcing lagging the commercial plan. It does not describe any prior Protiviti engagement with Meridian. [PV-08, The Pattern We See]
- The 'What Good Looks Like' practices are: independent validation of remediation closure before the programme is stood down; a control capacity plan linked to the growth plan, with volume triggers; governance as a precondition of deployment; and board reporting that separates management assertion from the independently validated position. These are generic Protiviti lessons, not tied to a named prior Meridian engagement. [PV-08, What Good Looks Like]
- Protiviti's catalogue lists financial crime, regulatory compliance and conduct services. These are capabilities, not evidence of delivery for Meridian. [PV-07, Risk and Compliance]
- The catalogue lists capabilities in cyber, operational resilience, third-party risk, internal audit, data and AI, and business performance improvement. It names no Meridian engagement. [PV-07, Technology and Cyber]

### Recommended Engagement Focus Areas

- Prioritise independent closure validation of Meridian's financial crime remediation. The board accepted second-line closure evidence with no mention of independent validation, and has already relied on that closure to raise the Institutional Services onboarding appetite. This is the strongest adjacent opportunity and the area most exposed to unvalidated assurance. [CL-05, Item 6 — Financial Crime Remediation Programme]
- Treat the remediation status as unresolved. The board minutes say 'substantially complete', while the annual report says workstreams continue into next year. The conflict cannot be settled from the evidence, and neither source says closure was independently validated. Engagement framing should avoid asserting either position and should use the gap itself as the case for independent validation. [CL-01, Section 5 — Statement on Remediation Activity]
- Pursue the FY2026 co-source decision. Meridian's plan requires about 1,300 audit days from co-source providers, mainly in cyber, cloud, financial crime and resilience, and no provider is named. PV-09 shows the client has not confirmed whether the arrangement will be extended, expanded or re-tendered. Protiviti should position quickly on this. [CL-09, Resourcing]
- Clarify the client's intent on the FY2026 co-source arrangement: whether it will be extended, expanded to financial crime, or re-tendered. This is the main unresolved commercial question, and it determines how the prior two-year term should be built on. [PV-09, Open Items at Time of Writing]
- Use the FY2024 audit themes as the evidence base for follow-on work. They include Asian entities behind on security baselines, repeatedly missed vulnerability remediation service levels, absent exit plans for critical suppliers, fragmented IAM, and optimistic modernisation reporting. Also note the relationship lessons: strong access to the Chief Internal Auditor and CTO, limited second-line financial crime access, and two scheduling disruptions. [PV-05, Principal Themes Reported]
- Focus on third-party exit planning and concentration risk. PV-05 found exit plans absent for two of three critical suppliers, and the board minutes confirm that plans exist for only one. The board has asked for a remediation plan by the end of Q1 2026. PV-09 notes that the work was scoped but not commissioned, and CL-09 carries the exit planning audit into FY2026. [CL-06, Item 5 — Third-Party Risk]
- Follow up on the sanctions screening tuning audit. It was deferred at client request in FY2024 and has again been carried into FY2026. Protiviti has a comparable payments institution credential to draw on. [CL-09, Prior Year Carry-Forward]
- Explore operational resilience dependency mapping. Third-party dependency mapping for the four most critical services is incomplete, and the board has asked for an update. The client planned to deliver this internally first, so timing and a support offer need testing. [CL-05, Item 8 — Operational Resilience]
- Develop the AI governance opportunity. It was discussed only exploratorily with the CTO. The FY2026 plan covers AI governance, but the readiness review is deferred until the framework exists. Protiviti's point of view stresses putting governance in place before deployment. [PV-09, Areas Discussed with the Client]
- Lead the closure validation pitch with the European bank credential, in which 9 of 41 findings were closed without sufficient evidence. Pair it with the Protiviti closure validation principle, under which evidence is tested independently rather than accepted on the basis of management assertion. [PV-03, Credential 1 — Financial Crime Remediation Validation, European Bank]
- Consider offering programme assurance on core platform modernisation. The programme is about four months behind schedule, and forecast spend has increased by GBP 14m. This echoes the FY2024 audit finding of optimistic milestone reporting and insufficient independent challenge. [CL-06, Item 4 — Core Platform Modernisation Programme]
- Build the answer on the Protiviti documents PV-01, PV-02 and PV-05, which record what was done and learned. Client and catalogue documents such as CL-01, CL-05, CL-06, CL-09 and PV-07 contain no Protiviti engagement history and should be used for context only. Verify the PV-01 2023 proposal and 2024 term against the PV-05 delivery record. [PV-01, Outcome]

## 2. Conflicts and Reconciliation

**Subject:** Status of the financial crime remediation programme (substantially complete vs. workstreams continuing into next year)

- **Source A:** Board was told the programme is 'substantially complete'. All twelve high-priority findings are remediated and closed, and the three open medium-priority findings are scheduled for closure within the quarter. [CL-05, Item 6 — Financial Crime Remediation Programme]
- **Source B:** Management says substantial progress has been made, but certain workstreams remain in delivery and will extend into the next financial year. This is more cautious than the board minutes. [CL-01, Section 5 — Statement on Remediation Activity]
- **Resolution basis:** unresolved
- **Resolution:** The findings give no dates for either document, so recency cannot be used. Both are Meridian-authored and neither is more independent or authoritative than the other. The two may also describe different scopes: the board minutes cover high-priority findings, while the annual statement speaks of a broader 'portfolio'. Neither source says closure was independently validated. The evidence does not support picking a side.
