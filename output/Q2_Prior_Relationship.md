# Research Pack: Where has Protiviti worked with Meridian before, and what did we learn?

## 1. Findings

### Prior engagement 1: Internal audit co-source (proposed 2023, term from 2024)

- Protiviti submitted a 2023 proposal to Meridian for internal audit co-source services covering technology, cyber and financial crime audit disciplines, with specialist resource across cyber, cloud, data and financial crime, QA and knowledge transfer. [PV-01, Client Context]
- Proposed scope included delivery of an agreed number of audits per annum from the approved internal audit plan, methodology aligned to the client's existing approach, and knowledge transfer to the in-house team. [PV-01, Proposed Scope]
- Blended day-rate model with an annual minimum commitment, a rate card by grade, and a flexible drawdown mechanism to accommodate plan changes during the year. [PV-01, Commercial Model]
- Differentiators presented: combined internal audit and specialist technology capability in one team, regulatory fluency across UK, Ireland, Luxembourg and Asia, and ability to flex specialist resource without renegotiation. [PV-01, Differentiators Presented]
- Protiviti was appointed for a two-year term commencing 2024 covering only a subset of the proposed disciplines; the financial crime audit scope was retained in-house at the client's election. [PV-01, Outcome]

### Prior engagement 2: FY2024 co-source delivery and lessons

- Year one of a two-year internal audit co-source term: 14 audits delivered against a planned 15; one audit deferred at client request. [PV-05, Engagement Summary]
- Audits delivered included cloud security configuration (UK and Luxembourg), group-wide identity and access management, vulnerability management, third-party onboarding and monitoring, data protection and cross-border transfers, change management over modernisation programme phase one, and eight further technology operations/infrastructure audits. [PV-05, Audits Delivered]
- The sanctions screening tuning audit was deferred at client request and carried forward. [PV-05, Deferred Audit]
- Principal themes reported: inconsistent security baselines across regional entities (Asian entities materially behind); weak vulnerability remediation with repeatedly missed service levels; third-party exit planning absent for two of three critical suppliers; fragmented IAM with no single view of privileged access; modernisation programme reporting showed optimistic milestones and insufficient independent challenge. [PV-05, Principal Themes Reported]
- Stakeholder engagement was strongest with the Chief Internal Auditor and CTO; access to the second-line financial crime team was limited because that scope was retained in-house; scheduling was disrupted twice by client resource availability. [PV-05, Relationship Observations]

### Prior engagement 3: Cyber security controls assessment (2025)

- Protiviti submitted a 2025 proposal to Meridian for a cyber security controls assessment across the Group estate, focused on cloud configuration and identity and access management. [PV-02, Client Context]
- Scope covered cyber control maturity against a recognised framework, cloud security baselines across UK, Luxembourg and Singapore, privileged access management and joiners/movers/leavers, vulnerability management service levels, and logging and monitoring coverage. [PV-02, Proposed Scope]
- Proposed team: engagement director, engagement manager, two cyber specialists and a cloud security specialist, supported by delivery centre resource for evidence analysis. [PV-02, Proposed Team Shape]
- Protiviti was appointed; fieldwork was conducted during 2025 and reporting was issued to the Technology and Operational Resilience Committee. [PV-02, Outcome]

### Follow-on opportunities and commercial position

- In 2025 Protiviti prepared a scoping note on potential support beyond the existing internal audit co-source arrangement. [PV-09, Background]
- Financial crime remediation closure validation was under consideration but deferred pending programme completion; operational resilience dependency mapping was to be delivered internally first; third-party exit planning for critical suppliers was scoped but not commissioned; AI governance framework design was discussed exploratorily with the CTO. [PV-09, Areas Discussed with the Client]
- Existing rate card applies; additional scope would be contracted as a separate SOW under the existing master services agreement. [PV-09, Commercial Position]
- The client had not confirmed whether the FY2026 co-source would be extended, expanded to include financial crime disciplines, or re-tendered. [PV-09, Open Items at Time of Writing]
- FY2026 audit plan expands coverage (financial crime, resilience, third-party, cloud security, AI governance) and needs specialist resource beyond current establishment; a co-source arrangement is being considered. This is a possible opening for Protiviti but no provider is named. [CL-06, Item 6 — Internal Audit Plan FY2026]
- Plan needs about 6,200 audit days against 4,900 internal capacity; the roughly 1,300-day shortfall is expected to be met via co-source arrangements, mainly in cyber, cloud, financial crime and resilience. No provider is named, so this does not confirm Protiviti's involvement. [CL-09, Resourcing]
- FY2026 plan includes a financial crime audit of 'closure validation of the remediation programme', which aligns with Protiviti's closure validation methodology and suggests the closure claims are to be independently tested. [CL-09, Thematic Coverage]
- Four FY2025 audits were carried into FY2026: sanctions screening tuning, Hong Kong entity governance, model risk management and third-party exit planning. AI governance readiness review is deferred pending a framework. [CL-09, Prior Year Carry-Forward]
- The plan comprises 38 audits and is risk-based, with reference to principal risks, supervisory priorities and prior audit findings. [CL-09, Planning Basis]
- Group Internal Audit (41 practitioners) has historically co-sourced specialist technology, cyber and financial crime audit work with external providers (none named). The Audit Committee has asked management to review the sustainability of the current co-source arrangement given the expanded FY2026 plan. This is the only indication of past external provider work in this document, and it does not identify Protiviti. [CL-03, Third Line Structure]

### Client-side context that bears on the lessons

- Board was told the financial crime remediation programme (initiated after the 2024 supervisory review) is 'substantially complete': all twelve high-priority findings remediated and closed with closure evidence accepted by the second line; three medium-priority findings remain open, scheduled for closure within the quarter. No Protiviti involvement is mentioned in this extract. [CL-05, Item 6 — Financial Crime Remediation Programme]
- Board approved an increase in the onboarding volume appetite threshold for Institutional Services on the basis that financial crime controls had been remediated and assessed as effective. This relies on management's assertion of closure, which is relevant to lessons on closure validation. [CL-05, Item 7 — Risk Appetite Review]
- Important business services identified and impact tolerances being set; third-party dependency mapping for the four most critical services remains incomplete. [CL-05, Item 8 — Operational Resilience]
- Group CRO is departing; external search to be initiated and interim arrangements approved. [CL-05, Item 9 — Chief Risk Officer Succession]
- Programme approximately four months behind baseline; data platform migration wave one slipped to Q2 2026; forecast spend up by GBP 14 million. [CL-06, Item 4 — Core Platform Modernisation Programme]
- Dependence on three critical technology suppliers; exit plans exist for only one; audit rights and incident notification provisions are inconsistent across suppliers. Board requested remediation plan by end of Q1 2026. [CL-06, Item 5 — Third-Party Risk]
- GBP 31 million provision recognised for the legacy product governance review in Retail Banking; affected customer population and final redress not yet determined. [CL-06, Item 8 — Consumer Outcomes]
- Group Head of Compliance Martin Ashby is accountable for the financial crime remediation portfolio. The financial crime function has the largest second-line vacancy gap (19 unfilled roles); second-line headcount is 214 against an establishment of 247. [CL-03, Second Line Structure]
- The CEO (Alanna Reyes) was appointed February 2026 with a mandate including completion of regulatory remediation. The CRO post has been vacant since November 2025, with Deputy CRO Hannah Okafor providing interim cover. [CL-03, Executive Committee]
- Group entities are in Dublin, Luxembourg, Singapore and Hong Kong. [CL-03, Regional Entities]

### Protiviti methodology and general lessons (not Meridian-specific engagement outcomes)

- Five-phase risk-based assurance approach: planning and risk assessment, design effectiveness, operating effectiveness, findings and root cause, reporting and follow-up. It is a generic methodology extract and names no Meridian engagement. [PV-04, Phase 1 — Planning and Risk Assessment]
- Protiviti treats closure validation as a distinct assurance activity. Evidence is tested independently against the original finding, and a finding is closed only where the underlying control is demonstrated to operate effectively, rather than on management assertion. [PV-04, Closure Validation Principle]
- Reporting goes to the accountable committee, and closure of actions is validated through independent evidence testing rather than management assertion. [PV-04, Phase 5 — Reporting and Follow-Up]
- Protiviti's generic point of view describes a pattern in which firms under-invest in control capacity during growth, including growth approved on the basis of a remediation programme reported as complete without independently validated closure evidence. The document does not name Meridian or describe any specific prior Protiviti engagement with Meridian; it carries the Meridian corpus header only. [PV-08, The Pattern We See]
- Three failure modes are listed: growth approved on a remediation programme reported complete without independent validation; control resourcing lagging the commercial plan by 12-18 months; governance forums constituted after deployment rather than before, notably for AI. These mirror issues seen elsewhere in the Meridian corpus, but the document does not tie them to a prior engagement. [PV-08, Three Failure Modes]
- Protiviti recommends independent validation of remediation closure before a programme is stood down, a control capacity plan linked to the commercial growth plan with volume triggers, governance established before deployment (notably for AI), and board reporting that separates management assertion from independently validated position. These are general lessons, not documented Meridian engagement outcomes. [PV-08, What Good Looks Like]

### Recommended Engagement Focus Areas

- Prioritise positioning Protiviti for the FY2026 financial crime 'closure validation of the remediation programme' audit. The board approved higher onboarding volume appetite on management's assertion that remediation was complete, and Protiviti's closure validation approach tests evidence independently rather than relying on management assertion. [CL-09, Thematic Coverage]
- Concentrate on the risk that the board relied on unvalidated remediation closure when raising the onboarding appetite. This is the clearest link between the past-engagement lessons and a live client need. [CL-05, Item 7 — Risk Appetite Review]
- Treat the FY2026 co-source extension, expansion or re-tender as the main commercial question. The client had not confirmed which route it would take, and the roughly 1,300-day capacity shortfall makes the decision pressing. [PV-09, Open Items at Time of Writing]
- Size the opportunity from the FY2026 plan's gap of about 6,200 audit days against 4,900 internal capacity. The gap is expected to be met by co-source in cyber, cloud, financial crime and resilience, but no provider is named, so Protiviti's share is unconfirmed. [CL-09, Resourcing]
- Revisit why the client kept financial crime audit in-house under the 2024 appointment. PV-05 records that this limited second-line access. The FY2026 plan now includes financial crime work, so the earlier limitation may be lifted. [PV-01, Outcome]
- Build forward-looking offers on the themes from FY2024 delivery: weak vulnerability remediation, missing third-party exit plans for two of three critical suppliers, fragmented IAM, and optimistic modernisation reporting. CL-06 shows the board still has concentration and exit-plan gaps and a delayed modernisation programme. [PV-05, Principal Themes Reported]
- Follow up on third-party exit planning. It was scoped but not commissioned, it was carried forward into FY2026, and the board asked for a remediation plan by end of Q1 2026. [CL-06, Item 5 — Third-Party Risk]
- Check whether the deferred sanctions screening tuning audit is still outstanding. It was deferred at client request in FY2024 and carried into FY2026, and it fits alongside the financial crime closure validation work. [CL-09, Prior Year Carry-Forward]
- Treat AI governance framework design as an early-stage opportunity. It was discussed only exploratorily with the CTO, and the readiness review is deferred until a framework exists. [PV-09, Areas Discussed with the Client]
- Plan stakeholder engagement around the CRO vacancy and the new CEO's remediation mandate. Past engagement was strongest with the Chief Internal Auditor and CTO, and the CRO post is now vacant with a new CEO in place. [CL-03, Executive Committee]
- Use Protiviti's growth-versus-control point of view to frame a conversation about the financial crime function's 19 unfilled second-line roles. The Meridian-specific link comes from CL-03. The point of view is generic and does not describe a prior Meridian engagement. [CL-03, Second Line Structure]
