### Lessons and implications for next engagement positioning

- Firms pursuing growth in regulated activity often under-invest in control capacity on the assumption that controls will scale. Control capacity is the binding constraint, and supervisors increasingly test whether capability was scaled ahead of volume. [PV-08, The Pattern We See]
- One failure mode is growth approved on the basis of a remediation programme reported as complete when closure evidence has not been independently validated. This mirrors the Meridian situation, in which board minutes claim that remediation is substantially complete. [PV-08, Three Failure Modes]
- Control resourcing plans lag the commercial plan by 12 to 18 months. [PV-08, Three Failure Modes]
- Governance forums are constituted after deployment rather than before, most visibly in AI adoption. [PV-08, Three Failure Modes]
- Good practice comprises four elements: independent validation of remediation closure before the programme is stood down; an explicit control capacity plan linked to the commercial plan, with volume triggers; governance established as a precondition of deployment; and board reporting that distinguishes management assertion from independently validated position. [PV-08, What Good Looks Like]
- Protiviti presented three differentiators: combined internal audit and specialist technology capability in one team; regulatory fluency across the UK, Ireland, Luxembourg and Asia; and the ability to flex specialist resource without renegotiation. [PV-01, Differentiators Presented]

## 2. Conflicts and Reconciliation

**Subject:** Status of Meridian's financial crime remediation programme (complete vs. still open findings)

- **Source A:** The Q3 2025 board minutes say the programme is 'substantially complete'. All twelve high-priority findings are reported as remediated and closed. Three medium-priority findings remain open, and no high-priority findings remain open. [CL-05, Item 6 — Financial Crime Remediation Programme]
- **Source B:** The press report says supervisors do not regard the remediation as complete. Four high-priority findings from the 2024 supervisory review remain open, including beneficial ownership verification and sanctions screening tuning. It also says some findings reported as closed were reopened after independent validation. [EX-07, Remediation Status Questioned]
- **Resolution basis:** unresolved
- **Resolution:** The two sources differ on both the count and the priority of open findings. The board minutes are a primary internal record but are management's own assertion. The press report cites an unnamed source close to the supervisor, so it is unverified and second-hand. Neither source clearly outranks the other, and their dates do not settle it. The annual report is softer than the board minutes, and the internal audit plan's deferred sanctions tuning audit is consistent with the press report. That supporting context does not prove the press account. The conflict should be presented to the reader as unresolved, with independent closure validation as the way to settle it.
