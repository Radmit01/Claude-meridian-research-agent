# Research Pack: Where has Protiviti worked with Meridian before, and what did we learn?

## 1. Findings

### Prior Protiviti engagements with Meridian

- Protiviti submitted a 2023 proposal to Meridian for internal audit co-source services covering technology, cyber and financial crime audit disciplines, offering specialist resource across cyber, cloud, data and financial crime. [PV-01, Client Context]
- Proposed scope: an agreed number of audits per annum from the approved internal audit plan, methodology alignment with the client's approach, and QA and knowledge transfer to the in-house team. [PV-01, Proposed Scope]
- Commercial model was a blended day-rate with annual minimum commitment, a rate card by grade, and a flexible drawdown mechanism to accommodate plan changes. [PV-01, Commercial Model]
- Protiviti was appointed for a two-year term commencing 2024 covering only a subset of the proposed disciplines; the financial crime audit scope was retained in-house at the client's election. [PV-01, Outcome]
- Differentiators presented: combined internal audit and specialist technology capability in one team, regulatory fluency across UK, Ireland, Luxembourg and Asia, and ability to flex specialist resource without renegotiation. [PV-01, Differentiators Presented]
- Protiviti submitted a 2025 proposal to Meridian for a cyber security controls assessment across the Group estate, focused on cloud configuration and identity and access management. [PV-02, Client Context]
- Scope: cyber maturity against a recognised framework, cloud security baselines across UK, Luxembourg and Singapore, privileged access management and joiners/movers/leavers, vulnerability management service levels, and logging/monitoring coverage. [PV-02, Proposed Scope]
- Proposed team: engagement director, engagement manager, two cyber specialists and a cloud security specialist, supported by delivery centre resource for evidence analysis. [PV-02, Proposed Team Shape]
- Protiviti was appointed; fieldwork was conducted during 2025 and reporting was issued to the Technology and Operational Resilience Committee. [PV-02, Outcome]
- Year one of the two-year co-source term delivered fourteen audits against fifteen planned; one audit was deferred at client request. [PV-05, Engagement Summary]
- Audits included cloud security configuration (UK and Luxembourg), group-wide IAM, vulnerability management, third-party onboarding and monitoring, data protection and cross-border transfers, change management over the modernisation programme phase one, plus eight further technology operations and infrastructure audits. [PV-05, Audits Delivered]
- The sanctions screening tuning audit was deferred at client request and carried forward. [PV-05, Deferred Audit]

### What we learned: findings themes from prior work

- Themes reported: inconsistent security baselines across regional entities with Asian entities materially behind; weak vulnerability remediation discipline with repeated SLA misses; absent third-party exit planning for two of three critical suppliers; fragmented IAM with no single view of privileged access; optimistic milestone reporting and insufficient independent challenge in modernisation programme assurance. [PV-05, Principal Themes Reported]
- The client's own document lists known technology control gaps: incomplete privileged access review coverage in the Asian entities; vulnerability remediation service levels missed in each of the last four quarters; logging and monitoring coverage not reaching all business-critical applications; and no consolidated inventory of models or AI-enabled tooling. It does not mention Protiviti. [CL-08, Known Technology Control Gaps]
- Supplier B (fund administration) and Supplier C (payments and screening) have no documented exit plan; Supplier C's contract has no right-to-audit clause; sanctions screening tuning was last reviewed in 2023. [CL-08, Critical Third-Party Suppliers]
- Exit plans exist for only one of three critical technology suppliers; audit rights and incident notification provisions are inconsistent. The Board requested a remediation plan by end of Q1 2026. [CL-06, Item 5 — Third-Party Risk]
- Mapping of third-party dependencies for the four most critical services remained incomplete, consistent with the third-party exit planning and resilience themes in Protiviti's prior work. [CL-05, Item 8 — Operational Resilience]
- The Risk Committee's FY2025 effectiveness review identified three gaps: inconsistent risk reporting across regional entities, limited aggregation of third-party risk data, and no defined framework for AI governance. The AI governance forum has not yet been constituted. The document does not mention Protiviti. [CL-07, Known Gaps]
- Stakeholder engagement was strongest with the Chief Internal Auditor and CTO; access to the second line financial crime team was limited because that scope was retained in-house; scheduling was disrupted twice by client resource availability. [PV-05, Relationship Observations]

This is the client's own assertion; Protiviti's independent validation was deferred (see PV-09). [CL-05, Item 6 — Financial Crime Remediation Programme]
- The Board approved increasing the onboarding-volume appetite threshold in Institutional Services on the basis that financial crime controls had been remediated and assessed as effective. [CL-05, Item 7 — Risk Appetite Review]
- A press report states supervisors do not regard the remediation as complete: four high-priority findings from the 2024 supervisory review remain open (beneficial ownership verification for complex fund structures, sanctions screening tuning), and some findings previously reported closed were reopened after independent validation found closure evidence insufficient. [EX-07, Remediation Status Questioned]
- The press report contrasts this with Q3 2025 board reporting describing the programme as substantially complete with all high-priority findings closed. UNRESOLVED CONFLICT: CL-05 is management's self-assessment without independent validation; EX-07 is a secondary press account that does not name its supervisory source, and the two may be counting different findings sets or different meetings. CL-01 and CL-08 are mildly consistent with EX-07 but do not establish closure status. Independent closure validation would resolve it. [EX-07, Remediation Status Questioned]
- Management states it continues to invest in remediating previously identified control weaknesses; substantial progress has been made across the financial crime remediation portfolio, but some workstreams remain in delivery and extend into the next financial year. The document does not mention Protiviti. [CL-01, Section 5 — Statement on Remediation Activity]
- The Group Head of Compliance, Martin Ashby, is accountable for the financial crime remediation portfolio, and the new CEO's mandate includes completing the regulatory remediation agenda. The Chief Internal Auditor is Rosalind Mbeki, in post since 2019. [CL-03, Executive Committee]
- Second line headcount was 214 against an establishment of 247, and the financial crime function has the largest vacancy gap at 19 roles. The document does not link this to any Protiviti work. [CL-03, Second Line Structure]

### Protiviti perspective, methodology and credentials (general, not Meridian-specific)

- Three failure modes: growth approved on a remediation programme reported complete without independently validated closure evidence; control resourcing lagging the commercial plan by 12–18 months; governance forums constituted after deployment (notably AI). This is general thought leadership and does not reference any specific Meridian engagement. [PV-08, Three Failure Modes]
- Good looks like: independent validation of remediation closure before stand-down; explicit control capacity plan linked to growth plan with volume triggers; governance as precondition of deployment; board reporting that distinguishes management assertion from independently validated position. [PV-08, What Good Looks Like]
- Protiviti's risk-based assurance approach treats closure validation as a distinct assurance activity: closure evidence is independently tested against the original finding, and a finding is closed only where the underlying control is demonstrated to operate effectively. This is a general methodology extract and describes no Meridian engagement. [PV-04, Closure Validation Principle]
- The approach has five phases: planning and risk assessment, design effectiveness, operating effectiveness, findings and root cause, and reporting and follow-up. Closure is validated through independent evidence testing rather than management assertion. The document names no Meridian engagement. [PV-04, Phase 5 — Reporting and Follow-Up]
- Credentials are sanitised and do not name Meridian; none can be attributed to Meridian from this document. They are labelled by client type (European Bank, UK Wealth Manager, Fund Administrator, etc.). [PV-03, Credential 1 — Financial Crime Remediation Validation, European Bank]
- Financial crime remediation validation: closure evidence tested for 41 findings; 9 had been closed without sufficient evidence and required reopening. [PV-03, Credential 1 — Financial Crime Remediation Validation, European Bank]
- Sanctions screening tuning review for a payments institution: independent review of screening configuration, fuzzy matching thresholds, and above/below-the-line testing, with recalibration recommendations. [PV-03, Credential 6 — Sanctions Screening Tuning Review, Payments Institution]

### Wider Meridian context

- The Board's principal risks include financial crime (AML and sanctions), operational resilience and third-party concentration, conduct in Retail Banking, data protection and cloud migration, technology change, and AI governance. Profit before tax fell to GBP 214m from GBP 241m, attributed partly to regulatory remediation costs and a GBP 31m legacy customer redress provision. [CL-01, Section 3 — Principal Risks and Uncertainties]
- The Committee is supported by four executive-level forums: the Group Risk Management Committee, the Financial Crime Committee, the Technology and Operational Resilience Committee, and the Conduct and Customer Outcomes Forum. [CL-07, Governance Structure Beneath the Committee]
- The analyst regards execution of the control agenda as the principal determinant of the equity story; regulatory overhang on financial crime is likely to constrain inorganic options and entity expansion approval. [EX-08, Implications for Meridian]

## 2. Conflicts and Reconciliation

**Subject:** Status of Meridian's financial crime remediation programme (complete vs. high-priority findings still open)

- **Source A:** The Board was told the programme is substantially complete. All twelve high-priority findings are remediated and closed, and three medium-priority findings remain open and are due to close within the quarter. This is management's own assertion. [CL-05, Item 6 — Financial Crime Remediation Programme]
- **Source B:** A press report says supervisors do not regard the remediation as complete. Four high-priority findings from the 2024 supervisory review remain open (beneficial ownership verification for complex fund structures, sanctions screening tuning). Some findings previously reported closed were reopened after independent validation found the closure evidence insufficient. [EX-07, Remediation Status Questioned]
- **Resolution basis:** unresolved
- **Resolution:** The evidence does not let us pick a side. CL-05 is a primary document, but it records management's self-assessment and no independent validation. PV-09 says Protiviti's independent validation was deferred, and PV-08 and PV-04 stress that management assertion is not validated closure. EX-07 is independent of management, but it is a secondary press account that does not name its supervisory source. It also refers to Q3 2025 board reporting, and the CL-05 meeting date is not confirmed to be the same one. The two documents may also be counting different things (all high-priority findings in the programme versus the 2024 supervisory review findings). CL-01 (some workstreams extend into next year) and CL-08 (sanctions screening tuning last reviewed in 2023) are mildly consistent with EX-07, but neither establishes closure status. There is no recency or authority basis for choosing one position, so this should be reported as an open conflict. Independent closure validation would resolve it.
