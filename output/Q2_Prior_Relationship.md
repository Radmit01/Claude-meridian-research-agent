# Research Pack: Where has Protiviti worked with Meridian before, and what did we learn?

## 1. Findings

### Prior Protiviti Engagements with Meridian

- Protiviti proposed internal audit co-source services to Meridian in 2023 covering technology, cyber and financial crime audit disciplines, and was appointed for a two-year term from 2024 covering a subset of the disciplines; financial crime audit scope was retained in-house at the client's election. [PV-01, Outcome]
- Proposed scope was an agreed number of audits per annum from the approved plan, methodology alignment, and QA/knowledge transfer; commercial model was blended day-rate with an annual minimum commitment and flexible drawdown. [PV-01, Commercial Model]
- Protiviti proposed a 2025 cyber security controls assessment across the Meridian Group estate (cloud configuration, IAM), was appointed, fieldwork was conducted in 2025 and reporting was issued to the Technology and Operational Resilience Committee. [PV-02, Outcome]
- Scope covered cyber control maturity, cloud baselines in UK, Luxembourg and Singapore, privileged access and joiners/movers/leavers, vulnerability management SLAs, and logging/monitoring coverage; team was an engagement director, manager, two cyber specialists and a cloud security specialist plus delivery centre support. [PV-02, Proposed Scope]
- In year one of the co-source term, fourteen audits were delivered against fifteen planned; one audit (sanctions screening tuning) was deferred at client request and carried forward. [PV-05, Engagement Summary]
- Audits included cloud security configuration (UK and Luxembourg), group-wide IAM, vulnerability management, third-party onboarding and monitoring, data protection and cross-border transfers, change management over modernisation programme phase one, and eight further technology operations/infrastructure audits. [PV-05, Audits Delivered]
- Independent validation of financial crime remediation closure (deferred pending programme completion), third-party exit planning (scoped, not commissioned), operational resilience dependency mapping (client to deliver internally first) and AI governance framework design (exploratory with the CTO) were discussed but not commissioned. [PV-09, Areas Discussed with the Client]
- The existing rate card applies and any additional scope would be a separate SOW under the existing master services agreement. [PV-09, Commercial Position]

### What We Learned: Findings Themes from FY2024 Audits

- Security baselines were applied inconsistently across regional entities, with Asian entities materially behind. [PV-05, Principal Themes Reported]
- Vulnerability remediation discipline was weak, with service levels missed repeatedly. [PV-05, Principal Themes Reported]
- Exit planning was absent for two of three critical suppliers, consistent with the Board's CL-06 observation that exit plans exist for only one of three. [PV-05, Principal Themes Reported]
- IAM was fragmented, with no single view of privileged access. [PV-05, Principal Themes Reported]
- Assurance found optimistic milestone reporting and insufficient independent challenge of status. [PV-05, Principal Themes Reported]

### What We Learned: Relationship Observations

- Engagement was strongest with the Chief Internal Auditor and CTO; second-line financial crime access was limited because that scope was retained in-house; scheduling was disrupted twice by client resource availability. [PV-05, Relationship Observations]
- At time of writing the client had not confirmed whether the FY2026 co-source would be extended, expanded to financial crime, or re-tendered. [PV-09, Open Items at Time of Writing]

### Meridian Context Bearing on the Prior Work

- The FY2026 plan expands to financial crime, operational resilience, third-party management, cloud security and AI governance; the Chief Internal Auditor said full delivery needs additional specialist resource and a co-source arrangement was being considered. [CL-06, Item 6 — Internal Audit Plan FY2026]
- The FY2026 plan expects a shortfall of about 1,300 audit days (6,200 required vs 4,900 internal capacity) to be met through co-source arrangements; the provider is not identified. [CL-09, Resourcing]
- Four FY2025 audits were deferred into FY2026: sanctions screening tuning, Hong Kong entity governance, model risk management and third-party exit planning. [CL-09, Prior Year Carry-Forward]
- The Board noted concentration risk from three critical technology suppliers, with exit plans for only one of three and inconsistent audit rights and incident notification; a remediation plan was requested by end Q1 2026. [CL-06, Item 5 — Third-Party Risk]
- Third-party dependency mapping for the four most critical important business services remained incomplete. [CL-05, Item 8 — Operational Resilience]
- No defined AI governance framework exists and the AI governance forum has not been constituted. [CL-07, Known Gaps]
- Second line headcount was 214 vs an establishment of 247, with financial crime carrying the largest gap (19 roles); the Group CRO post has been vacant since November 2025. [CL-03, Second Line Structure]

### Financial Crime Remediation Status (Unresolved Conflict)

- Q3 2025 board minutes state the programme is substantially complete, with all twelve high-priority findings closed and three medium-priority findings open. [CL-05, Item 6 — Financial Crime Remediation Programme]
- A press report states four high-priority findings remain open and that some findings reported as closed were reopened after independent validation testing found closure evidence insufficient; the report does not say who performed that testing. This conflict is unresolved: the board minutes are management self-report and the press account relies on an anonymous source. [EX-07, Remediation Status Questioned]
- The FY2025 annual report says substantial progress has been made with some workstreams extending into the next year, which is consistent with either account. [CL-01, Section 5 — Statement on Remediation Activity]
- The Board raised Institutional Services onboarding volume appetite on the basis that financial crime controls were remediated and effective, so the status dispute bears directly on that decision. [CL-05, Item 7 — Risk Appetite Review]

### Protiviti Methodology, Credentials and Point of View (Generic, Not Meridian-Specific)

- Protiviti's methodology treats closure validation as a distinct assurance activity, testing closure evidence independently and regarding a finding as closed only where the control is demonstrated to operate effectively. This is a generic extract and does not describe Meridian work. [PV-04, Closure Validation Principle]
- Protiviti's point of view lists failure modes including growth approved on a remediation programme reported as complete without independent validation, control resourcing lagging the commercial plan by 12 to 18 months, and governance forums constituted after deployment. The document does not mention Meridian. [PV-08, Three Failure Modes]
- The six credentials (including a financial crime remediation validation where 9 of 41 findings were reopened) are sanitised and cannot be tied to Meridian; they show experience at other firms only. [PV-03, Credential 1 — Financial Crime Remediation Validation, European Bank]
- The catalogue lists generic Protiviti capabilities (independent closure validation, co-source, AI governance, third-party risk) with no statement that any were delivered to Meridian. [PV-07, Risk and Compliance]

### Recommended Engagement Focus Areas

- Prioritise scoping independent validation of financial crime remediation closure. The client deferred it pending completion, yet the board and the press disagree on whether high-priority findings are closed, and the board raised risk appetite on the assumption of effective controls. Independent evidence testing is the only way to resolve the conflict. [CL-05, Item 7 — Risk Appetite Review]
- Focus on the FY2026 co-source decision (extend, expand to financial crime, or re-tender). Meridian has a ~1,300-day capacity shortfall and a Chief Internal Auditor seeking specialist support, while PV-05 shows a delivery record of 14 of 15 audits and strongest relationships with the CIA and CTO. [CL-09, Resourcing]
- Re-engage on third-party exit planning for critical suppliers. It was scoped but not commissioned, our FY2024 audit found exit plans missing for two of three suppliers, and the Board requested a remediation plan by end Q1 2026. [PV-05, Principal Themes Reported]
- Revisit operational resilience dependency mapping support. The client planned internal delivery first, but the board minutes show mapping for the four most critical services is still incomplete. [CL-05, Item 8 — Operational Resilience]
- Position AI governance framework design as a near-term opportunity: it was discussed exploratorily with the CTO (a strong relationship), and Meridian has no framework and an unconstituted governance forum. [CL-07, Known Gaps]
- Raise the deferred sanctions screening tuning audit, which Protiviti deferred at client request in FY2024 and which remains carried forward into FY2026. It also overlaps with the open sanctions findings reported in the press. [CL-09, Prior Year Carry-Forward]
- Plan to broaden stakeholder access beyond the CIA and CTO, particularly to the second-line financial crime team, and to schedule around client resource constraints. FY2024 experience showed limited financial crime access and two scheduling disruptions. [PV-05, Relationship Observations]

## 2. Conflicts and Reconciliation

**Subject:** Status of the financial crime remediation programme: complete, or high-priority findings still open

- **Source A:** Q3 2025 board minutes state the programme is substantially complete. All twelve high-priority findings are remediated and closed. Three medium-priority findings remain open and are due for closure in the current quarter. [CL-05, Item 6 — Financial Crime Remediation Programme]
- **Source B:** A press report says four high-priority findings from the 2024 supervisory review remain open, including beneficial ownership verification and sanctions screening tuning. It says supervisors do not regard the programme as complete. An unnamed person close to the supervisor says some findings reported as closed were reopened after independent validation testing found the closure evidence insufficient. [EX-07, Remediation Status Questioned]
- **Resolution basis:** unresolved
- **Resolution:** The evidence does not let us pick a side. The board minutes are management's own self-report. The press report relies on an anonymous source and does not say who did the validation testing. Recency slightly favours EX-07, because it comes after the Q3 2025 minutes and mentions Q4 2025 events. However, it is unclear whether the findings were reopened after the board meeting or whether the board was simply wrong, and no primary supervisor or validation document is available. CL-01 (FY2025 annual report) is softer, saying 'substantial progress' with some workstreams extending into the next year. That is consistent with either account and does not settle it. The discrepancy should be reported as an open conflict. It matters for the deferred independent closure validation work (PV-09) and for the board's decision to raise its risk appetite (CL-05, Item 7).
