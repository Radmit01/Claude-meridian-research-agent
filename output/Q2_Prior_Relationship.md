# Research Pack: Where has Protiviti worked with Meridian before, and what did we learn?

## 1. Findings

### Prior Protiviti engagements with Meridian: internal audit co-source (2023 proposal, 2024–2026 delivery)

- Protiviti submitted a 2023 proposal to Meridian for internal audit co-source services covering technology, cyber and financial crime audit disciplines. The proposal comprised specialist resource across cyber, cloud, data and financial crime; an agreed number of audits per annum; methodology alignment; and QA and knowledge transfer. [PV-01, Client Context]
- Proposed scope: specialist audit resource across cyber, cloud, data and financial crime; delivery of an agreed number of audits per annum from the approved plan; methodology alignment to the client's approach; and QA and knowledge transfer to the in-house team. [PV-01, Proposed Scope]
- The commercial model was a blended day-rate with an annual minimum commitment, a rate card by grade, and a flexible drawdown mechanism to accommodate plan changes. [PV-01, Commercial Model]
- Outcome and lesson (partial win): Protiviti was appointed for a two-year term from 2024 covering only a subset of the proposed disciplines. Financial crime audit scope was retained in-house at the client's election. [PV-01, Outcome]
- Differentiators presented: a combined internal audit and specialist technology capability in one team; regulatory fluency across the UK, Ireland, Luxembourg and Asia; and the ability to flex specialist resource without renegotiation. [PV-01, Differentiators Presented]
- In year one of the two-year internal audit co-source, fourteen audits were delivered against a planned fifteen; one audit was deferred at the client's request. [PV-05, Engagement Summary]
- Audits delivered included cloud security configuration (UK and Luxembourg), group-wide IAM, vulnerability management, third-party onboarding and monitoring, data protection and cross-border transfers, and change management over phase one of the modernisation programme, plus eight further technology operations and infrastructure audits. [PV-05, Audits Delivered]
- The sanctions screening tuning audit was deferred at the client's request and carried forward. [PV-05, Deferred Audit]

### Prior Protiviti engagements with Meridian: cyber security assessment (2025)

- Protiviti submitted a 2025 proposal for an assessment of cyber security controls across the Meridian Group estate, focused on cloud configuration and identity and access management. [PV-02, Client Context]
- Scope: cyber control maturity against a recognised framework; cloud security baselines across the UK, Luxembourg and Singapore; privileged access management and joiners/movers/leavers; vulnerability management service levels; and logging and monitoring coverage against the critical application inventory. [PV-02, Proposed Scope]
- Proposed team: an engagement director, an engagement manager, two cyber specialists and a cloud security specialist, supported by delivery centre resource for evidence analysis. [PV-02, Proposed Team Shape]
- Protiviti was appointed; fieldwork was conducted in 2025 and reporting was issued to the Technology and Operational Resilience Committee. [PV-02, Outcome]

### What we learned: audit findings and relationship observations from Meridian work

- Themes reported: inconsistent security baselines across regional entities (Asian entities materially behind); weak vulnerability remediation, with repeated missed SLAs; absent third-party exit planning for two of three critical suppliers; fragmented IAM, with no single view of privileged access; and, in modernisation programme assurance, optimistic milestone reporting and insufficient independent challenge. [PV-05, Principal Themes Reported]
- Relationship: the engagement was strongest with the Chief Internal Auditor and the CTO; access to the second-line financial crime team was limited because that scope was retained in-house; and scheduling was disrupted twice by client resource availability. [PV-05, Relationship Observations]
- Outcome and lesson (partial win): Protiviti was appointed for a two-year term from 2024 covering only a subset of the proposed disciplines. Financial crime audit scope was retained in-house at the client's election. [PV-01, Outcome]

- Protiviti redesigned the supplier lifecycle (criticality assessment, contractual minimum provisions, exit planning and concentration analysis) across more than 400 vendors. This matches Meridian's third-party concentration and exit-plan gaps. [PV-03, Credential 3 — Third-Party Risk Management Uplift, Fund Administrator]
- This was a multi-year co-source covering technology, cyber, cloud and financial crime audits across five jurisdictions, averaging 22 audits per annum. It matches Meridian's co-source need. [PV-03, Credential 4 — Internal Audit Co-Source, Institutional Services Group]
- Protiviti designed an AI governance framework comprising a use case inventory, risk classification, human oversight requirements, and integration with existing model risk management. [PV-03, Credential 5 — AI Governance Framework, Asset Manager]
- This was an independent review of screening configuration, fuzzy matching thresholds, and above-the-line and below-the-line testing, with recalibration recommendations. It relates to Meridian's open sanctions screening tuning finding. [PV-03, Credential 6 — Sanctions Screening Tuning Review, Payments Institution]
- The document is a generic Protiviti methodology extract describing a five-phase, risk-based assurance approach: planning and risk assessment, design effectiveness, operating effectiveness, findings and root cause, and reporting and follow-up. It does not identify any specific prior Protiviti engagement with Meridian, nor any client, dates or scope. [PV-04, Phase 1 — Planning and Risk Assessment]
- Protiviti's approach treats closure validation as a distinct assurance activity: evidence of closure is tested independently against the original finding, and a finding is regarded as closed only where the underlying control is demonstrated to operate effectively. This is a methodological principle that may inform lessons learned, but it is not stated as a lesson from a Meridian engagement. [PV-04, Closure Validation Principle]
- In reporting and follow-up, Protiviti reports to the accountable committee and validates closure of actions through independent evidence testing rather than management assertion. [PV-04, Phase 5 — Reporting and Follow-Up]
- Findings are rated by risk, the root cause (rather than the symptom) is identified, and management actions are agreed with defined owners and dates. [PV-04, Phase 4 — Findings and Root Cause]
- Operating effectiveness testing uses risk-based sampling, analytics over full populations where data permits, and evidence validation. [PV-04, Phase 3 — Operating Effectiveness]
