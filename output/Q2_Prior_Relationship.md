# Research Pack: Where has Protiviti worked with Meridian before, and what did we learn?

## 1. Findings

### Prior Protiviti engagements with Meridian

- In 2023, Protiviti submitted a proposal to Meridian for internal audit co-source services covering the technology, cyber and financial crime audit disciplines. [PV-01, Client Context]
- The proposed scope comprised: specialist audit resource across cyber, cloud, data and financial crime; an agreed number of audits per year drawn from the approved audit plan; methodology alignment with the client's approach; and quality assurance and knowledge transfer to the in-house team. [PV-01, Proposed Scope]
- The commercial model was a blended day rate with an annual minimum commitment, a rate card by grade, and a flexible drawdown mechanism to accommodate changes to the plan. [PV-01, Commercial Model]
- Protiviti's pitch emphasised three differentiators: combined internal audit and specialist technology capability within a single team; regulatory fluency across the UK, Ireland, Luxembourg and Asia; and the ability to flex specialist resource without renegotiation. [PV-01, Differentiators Presented]
- Protiviti was appointed for a two-year term commencing in 2024, covering only a subset of the proposed disciplines. At the client's election, the financial crime audit scope was retained in-house. [PV-01, Outcome]
- In 2025, Protiviti submitted a proposal to assess cyber security controls across the Meridian Group estate, with a focus on cloud configuration and identity and access management. [PV-02, Client Context]
- The proposed scope covered: cyber control maturity against a recognised framework; cloud security baselines in the UK, Luxembourg and Singapore; privileged access management and joiners/movers/leavers processes; vulnerability management service levels; and logging and monitoring coverage against the critical application inventory. [PV-02, Proposed Scope]
- The proposed team comprised an engagement director, an engagement manager, two cyber specialists and a cloud security specialist, supported by delivery centre resource for evidence analysis. [PV-02, Proposed Team Shape]
- Protiviti was appointed. Fieldwork was conducted during 2025, and reporting was issued to the Technology and Operational Resilience Committee. [PV-02, Outcome]
- In 2025, Protiviti prepared a scoping note/SOW extract on potential support beyond the existing internal audit co-source arrangement. [PV-09, Background]

### Delivery record: FY2024 co-source

- In year one of the two-year internal audit co-source (FY2024), fourteen audits were delivered against fifteen planned; one audit was deferred at the client's request. [PV-05, Engagement Summary]
- The audits delivered covered: cloud security configuration (UK and Luxembourg); group-wide identity and access management; vulnerability management; third-party onboarding and monitoring; data protection and cross-border transfers; and change management over phase one of the modernisation programme. Eight further technology operations and infrastructure audits were also delivered. [PV-05, Audits Delivered]
- The sanctions screening tuning audit was deferred at the client's request and carried forward. [PV-05, Deferred Audit]

### What we learned: findings and relationship

- The key themes reported were: inconsistent security baselines across regional entities, with Asian entities materially behind; weak vulnerability remediation, with SLAs repeatedly missed; absent third-party exit planning for two of three critical suppliers; fragmented identity and access management, with no single view of privileged access; and, in the modernisation programme assurance, optimistic milestone reporting and insufficient independent challenge. [PV-05, Principal Themes Reported]
- Engagement was strongest with the Chief Internal Auditor and the CTO. Access to the second-line financial crime team was limited because that scope was retained in-house, and scheduling was disrupted twice by client resource availability. [PV-05, Relationship Observations]
- The financial crime audit scope was retained in-house at the client's election, which limited Protiviti's access to the second-line financial crime team. [PV-01, Outcome]

### Opportunities discussed but not converted

- Areas discussed: independent validation of financial crime remediation closure was under consideration but deferred pending programme completion; operational resilience dependency mapping was to be delivered internally first; third-party exit planning was scoped but not commissioned; and AI governance framework design was discussed on an exploratory basis with the CTO. [PV-09, Areas Discussed with the Client]
- The existing rate card applies. Any additional scope would be contracted as a separate SOW under the existing master services agreement. [PV-09, Commercial Position]
- The client had not confirmed whether the FY2026 co-source would be extended, expanded to include financial crime disciplines, or re-tendered. [PV-09, Open Items at Time of Writing]
- The Chief Internal Auditor said that full delivery of the FY2026 audit plan requires additional specialist resource beyond the current establishment, and that a co-source arrangement was being considered. The plan covers financial crime, operational resilience, third-party management, cloud security and AI governance. [CL-06, Item 6 — Internal Audit Plan FY2026]
- The plan requires about 6,200 audit days against 4,900 days of internal capacity. The shortfall of about 1,300 days is expected to be met through co-source arrangements, mainly in cyber, cloud, financial crime and resilience. [CL-09, Resourcing]
- The plan covers financial crime (including closure validation of the remediation programme), operational resilience, third-party risk (exit planning and concentration) and AI governance. The AI governance readiness review is deferred pending establishment of the framework. [CL-09, Thematic Coverage]
- Four FY2025 audits were carried into FY2026: sanctions screening tuning, Hong Kong entity governance, model risk management and third-party exit planning. [CL-09, Prior Year Carry-Forward]

### Client context reinforcing the lessons

- The Board noted concentration risk arising from dependence on three critical technology suppliers. Exit plans exist for only one of the three, and audit-rights and incident-notification provisions are inconsistent. The Board requested a remediation plan by the end of Q1 2026. [CL-06, Item 5 — Third-Party Risk]
- Mapping of third-party dependencies for the four most critical services remained incomplete. This links to the dependency mapping and third-party exit planning areas discussed with Protiviti. [CL-05, Item 8 — Operational Resilience]
- The Board approved an increased onboarding volume appetite in Institutional Services on the basis that financial crime controls had been remediated and assessed as effective. [CL-05, Item 7 — Risk Appetite Review]

### Financial crime remediation status (unresolved conflict)

- The Board was told that the financial crime remediation programme is substantially complete: all twelve high-priority findings have been remediated and closed, and three medium-priority findings remain open. This is relevant to the deferred independent validation of closure in PV-09. [CL-05, Item 6 — Financial Crime Remediation Programme]
- Persons familiar with the supervisory dialogue say that four high-priority findings from the 2024 supervisory review remain open, including beneficial ownership verification for complex fund structures and sanctions screening tuning. Some findings reported as closed were reportedly reopened after independent validation testing found the closure evidence insufficient. [EX-07, Remediation Status Questioned]
- UNRESOLVED: CL-05 is management's own assertion, and EX-07 relies on unnamed sources. Neither constitutes independent validation, and no other document confirms either count. The reopenings described in EX-07 may postdate the board reporting, so the evidence does not support picking a side. Closure validation was deferred per PV-08 and PV-09. [EX-07, Remediation Status Questioned]
- Analysts noted that the discrepancy would echo a peer enforcement action in which inaccurate internal reporting to the board was an aggravating factor. In its investor presentation, the Group described remediation as 'well advanced and moving to completion', and it declined to comment on supervisory correspondence. [EX-07, Remediation Status Questioned]

### Transferable lessons from Protiviti credentials and thought leadership (not Meridian work)

- The credentials describe work for other clients (European Bank, UK Wealth Manager, Fund Administrator, Institutional Services Group, Asset Manager and Payments Institution). None is identified as Meridian, so this document does not evidence prior Meridian work. [PV-03, Credential 1 — Financial Crime Remediation Validation, European Bank]
- Independent validation tested closure evidence for 41 findings. Nine had been closed without sufficient evidence and required reopening. A validated closure position was delivered to the board and the supervisor. [PV-03, Credential 1 — Financial Crime Remediation Validation, European Bank]
- The work covered important business services, impact tolerances, resource and third-party dependency mapping, and scenario testing design. [PV-03, Credential 2 — Operational Resilience Implementation, UK Wealth Manager]
- The supplier lifecycle redesign covered criticality assessment, contractual minimum provisions, exit planning and concentration analysis across more than 400 vendors. [PV-03, Credential 3 — Third-Party Risk Management Uplift, Fund Administrator]
- A multi-year co-source covered technology, cyber, cloud and financial crime audits across five jurisdictions, averaging 22 audits per year. [PV-03, Credential 4 — Internal Audit Co-Source, Institutional Services Group]
- An AI governance framework was designed with a use-case inventory, risk classification, human oversight requirements and integration with model risk management. [PV-03, Credential 5 — AI Governance Framework, Asset Manager]
- The work comprised an independent review of screening configuration, fuzzy matching thresholds and above/below-the-line testing, with recalibration recommendations. [PV-03, Credential 6 — Sanctions Screening Tuning Review, Payments Institution]
- Firms pursuing growth often under-invest in control capacity on the assumption that existing controls will scale. Control capacity then becomes the binding constraint, and supervisors test whether capability was scaled ahead of volume. [PV-08, The Pattern We See]
- Three failure modes were identified: growth approved on a remediation programme reported complete without independent closure validation; control resourcing lagging the commercial plan by 12–18 months; and governance forums set up after deployment, most visibly for AI. [PV-08, Three Failure Modes]
- Good practice comprises: independent validation of remediation closure before stand-down; a control capacity plan linked to the growth plan with volume triggers; governance as a precondition of deployment; and board reporting that distinguishes management assertion from independently validated position. [PV-08, What Good Looks Like]

### Recommended Engagement Focus Areas

- Prioritise offering independent validation of the financial crime remediation closure. The board-reported status (all 12 high-priority findings closed) and the press account (4 still open) are unresolved, and neither source is independent. Closure validation was explicitly deferred, so this is the clearest unconverted opportunity and the best place to apply the lessons from earlier work. [PV-09, Areas Discussed with the Client]
- Treat the CL-05 vs EX-07 conflict as unresolved, and do not pick a side in client-facing material. The resolution note says the reopenings described in EX-07 may postdate the board reporting. Frame the conflict as the reason for independent validation, not as an accusation. [EX-07, Remediation Status Questioned]
- Examine the board's increased onboarding volume appetite, which was approved on the premise that financial crime controls were remediated and effective. If the remediation status is wrong, this decision is exposed. This gives a strong, evidence-based reason to raise validation with the Board and Chief Internal Auditor. [CL-05, Item 7 — Risk Appetite Review]
- Concentrate on the FY2026 co-source decision (extend, expand to financial crime, or re-tender). The client has not confirmed which option it will take, and the Chief Internal Auditor says specialist resource is needed. The shortfall is about 1,300 days, mainly in cyber, cloud, financial crime and resilience. This is the commercial window. [PV-09, Open Items at Time of Writing]
- Size a proposal against the roughly 1,300-day gap between the 6,200 audit days needed and the 4,900 days of internal capacity. This gap shows concrete demand that matches the disciplines Protiviti already delivers. [CL-09, Resourcing]
- Pursue third-party exit planning and concentration risk. Our FY2024 audit found exit plans absent for two of three critical suppliers. The board asked for a remediation plan by end of Q1 2026, and the audit was carried into FY2026. It was scoped earlier but not commissioned. The finding supports a pitch that builds on our prior audit work. [PV-05, Principal Themes Reported]
- Link the deferred sanctions screening tuning audit to the open-findings dispute. EX-07 names sanctions screening tuning among the findings still open; the audit was deferred at the client's request and carried into FY2026; and Protiviti holds a relevant sanctions screening credential. [CL-09, Prior Year Carry-Forward]
- Offer support on operational resilience dependency mapping. The board noted that mapping for the four most critical services was incomplete, even though the client had planned to deliver it internally first. This gap suggests a reason to revisit the offer. [CL-05, Item 8 — Operational Resilience]
- Position AI governance framework design as an early-stage opportunity. It was discussed exploratorily with the CTO, and the FY2026 plan defers the readiness review until the framework exists. The timing is supported by Protiviti's AI governance credential and by its point of view that governance forums are often set up after deployment. [CL-09, Thematic Coverage]
- Use the relationship lessons when shaping the next engagement. Engagement was strongest with the Chief Internal Auditor and the CTO; access to second-line financial crime was limited by the in-house scope; and scheduling was disrupted twice by client availability. Expanding into financial crime would resolve the access limit. Resourcing and scheduling protocols should be agreed up front. [PV-05, Relationship Observations]
- Understand why the client kept financial crime audit scope in-house in 2023–24. Any push to widen the scope should address that decision directly, and the proof point is the validation credential, in which 9 of 41 closures lacked sufficient evidence. [PV-01, Outcome]
- Lead with the European Bank validation credential as evidence that management-reported closure can overstate the position. In that engagement, 9 of 41 closed findings were reopened. The credential supports the case for validating Meridian's claim. It is a sanitised credential about another client, however, and is not evidence of prior Meridian work. [PV-03, Credential 1 — Financial Crime Remediation Validation, European Bank]
- Build the pitch around the point of view's good-practice pattern, in which board reporting separates management assertion from independently validated position. This pattern maps directly onto the CL-05 and EX-07 discrepancy and the peer enforcement precedent. [PV-08, What Good Looks Like]

## 2. Conflicts and Reconciliation

**Subject:** Status of the financial crime remediation programme: whether all high-priority findings are closed

- **Source A:** The board was told the programme is substantially complete: all twelve high-priority findings were remediated and closed, and three medium-priority findings remain open. [CL-05, Item 6 — Financial Crime Remediation Programme]
- **Source B:** Anonymous sources familiar with the supervisory dialogue say four high-priority findings from the 2024 supervisory review remain open. These include beneficial ownership verification and sanctions screening tuning. Some findings reported as closed were reportedly reopened after independent validation found the closure evidence insufficient. [EX-07, Remediation Status Questioned]
- **Resolution basis:** unresolved
- **Resolution:** Neither source is clearly more authoritative or more recent. CL-05 is management's own assertion to the board, and the board's risk appetite decision in Item 7 relied on it. EX-07 is a press report that relies on unnamed sources, so it is not an independent validation. No other document, such as a validated closure report or the confidential supervisory annex referenced in CL-06, confirms either count. The two accounts are about the same period (Q3 2025 board reporting versus the later press report). That gap does not show which is correct, because the reopenings EX-07 describes may postdate the board reporting. The evidence does not support picking a side, so the conflict is flagged for independent closure validation. PV-08 and PV-09 note that closure validation was deferred.
