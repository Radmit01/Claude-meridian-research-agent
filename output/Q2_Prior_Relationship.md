# Research Pack: Where has Protiviti worked with Meridian before, and what did we learn?

## 1. Findings

### Prior Protiviti Engagements with Meridian

- Protiviti submitted a 2023 proposal to Meridian for internal audit co-source services covering technology, cyber and financial crime audit disciplines. [PV-01, Client Context]
- Proposed scope: specialist audit resource across cyber, cloud, data and financial crime; agreed number of audits per annum from the approved IA plan; methodology alignment; QA and knowledge transfer to the in-house team. [PV-01, Proposed Scope]
- Blended day-rate model with annual minimum commitment, rate card by grade, and a flexible drawdown mechanism to accommodate plan changes during the year. [PV-01, Commercial Model]
- Protiviti was appointed for a two-year term commencing 2024 covering a subset of the proposed disciplines; financial crime audit scope was retained in-house at the client's election. [PV-01, Outcome]
- Differentiators presented: combined internal audit and specialist technology capability in one team; regulatory fluency across UK, Ireland, Luxembourg and Asia; ability to flex specialist resource without renegotiation. [PV-01, Differentiators Presented]
- Protiviti submitted a 2025 proposal for an assessment of cyber security controls across the Meridian Group estate, focused on cloud configuration and identity and access management. [PV-02, Client Context]
- Scope: cyber control maturity against a recognised framework; cloud security baselines across UK, Luxembourg and Singapore; privileged access management and joiners/movers/leavers; vulnerability management service levels; logging and monitoring coverage against business-critical applications. [PV-02, Proposed Scope]
- Team: engagement director, engagement manager, two cyber specialists, a cloud security specialist, supported by delivery centre resource for evidence analysis. [PV-02, Proposed Team Shape]
- Protiviti was appointed; fieldwork was conducted in 2025 and reporting was issued to the Technology and Operational Resilience Committee. [PV-02, Outcome]
- Protiviti already has an internal audit co-source arrangement with Meridian; the 2025 scoping note concerned potential support beyond it. [PV-09, Background]

### What We Learned: FY2024 Co-Source Delivery

- Year one of the two-year co-source term: fourteen audits delivered against a planned fifteen; one audit deferred at client request. [PV-05, Engagement Summary]
- Audits covered cloud security configuration (UK, Luxembourg), group-wide IAM, vulnerability management, third-party onboarding and monitoring, data protection and cross-border transfers, change management over modernisation programme phase one, plus eight further technology operations and infrastructure audits. [PV-05, Audits Delivered]
- Themes reported: inconsistent security baselines across regional entities with Asian entities materially behind; weak vulnerability remediation with repeatedly missed service levels; absent third-party exit planning for two of three critical suppliers; fragmented IAM with no single view of privileged access; modernisation programme assurance found optimistic milestone reporting and insufficient independent challenge. [PV-05, Principal Themes Reported]
- Stakeholder engagement was strongest with the Chief Internal Auditor and CTO; access to the second line financial crime team was limited since that scope was retained in-house; scheduling was disrupted twice by client resource availability. [PV-05, Relationship Observations]
- The sanctions screening tuning audit was deferred at client request and carried forward. [PV-05, Deferred Audit]

### Discussed but Not Commissioned

- Independent validation of financial crime remediation closure was under consideration but deferred by the client pending completion of the programme (not commissioned). [PV-09, Areas Discussed with the Client]
- Operational resilience dependency mapping support was discussed; the client indicated internal delivery in the first instance (not commissioned). [PV-09, Areas Discussed with the Client]
- Third-party exit planning for critical suppliers was scoped but not commissioned. [PV-09, Areas Discussed with the Client]
- AI governance framework design was discussed at an exploratory level with the Chief Technology Officer. [PV-09, Areas Discussed with the Client]
- The existing rate card applies. Any additional scope would be contracted as a separate SOW under the existing master services agreement. [PV-09, Commercial Position]
- The client had not confirmed whether the FY2026 co-source would be extended, expanded to financial crime disciplines, or re-tendered. This is the main open commercial question. [PV-09, Open Items at Time of Writing]

### Client Context Relevant to the Lessons

- The Board was told the programme was substantially complete: all 12 high-priority findings were remediated and closed with closure evidence accepted by the second line, and 3 medium-priority findings remained open. The Chair asked for preparation for formal closure. Protiviti's independent closure validation was deferred (PV-09) and not performed. [CL-05, Item 6 — Financial Crime Remediation Programme]
- The Board raised the onboarding-volume appetite threshold in Institutional Services on the basis that financial crime controls were remediated and effective. Without independent validation, this rests on the client's own assertion. [CL-05, Item 7 — Risk Appetite Review]
- The Group had identified its important business services and was setting impact tolerances; third-party dependency mapping for the four most critical services was incomplete. This is the area where Protiviti offered mapping support, which the client said it would deliver internally first. [CL-05, Item 8 — Operational Resilience]
- Management states substantial progress has been made across the financial crime remediation portfolio, with certain workstreams extending into the next financial year. This is management's own assertion and is not independently validated. [CL-01, Section 5 — Statement on Remediation Activity]
- The Board identifies principal risks including financial crime (AML/sanctions, especially Institutional Services), operational resilience (third-party concentration), conduct in Retail Banking, data protection/cloud migration, technology change, and emerging AI governance risk. [CL-01, Section 3 — Principal Risks and Uncertainties]
- Profit before tax fell to GBP 214m from GBP 241m, attributed principally to elevated regulatory remediation costs, technology investment, and a GBP 31m legacy customer redress provision in Retail Banking. [CL-01, Section 2 — Financial Highlights]
- Group Internal Audit (41 practitioners) has historically co-sourced specialist technology, cyber and financial crime audit work with external providers, and the Audit Committee has asked management to review the sustainability of the co-source arrangement given the expanded FY2026 plan. The providers are not named, so no link to Protiviti is established by this document. [CL-03, Third Line Structure]
- Second line headcount was 214 against an approved establishment of 247, with the largest vacancy gap in financial crime (19 unfilled roles). [CL-03, Second Line Structure]
- The Group CRO position has been vacant since November 2025 (interim cover by Deputy CRO Hannah Okafor); new CEO Alanna Reyes was appointed February 2026 with a mandate including completion of the regulatory remediation agenda; Martin Ashby (Group Head of Compliance) is accountable for the financial crime remediation portfolio. [CL-03, Executive Committee]

### Protiviti Methodology and Credentials (Not Meridian-Specific)

- Protiviti treats closure validation as a distinct assurance activity: evidence of closure is tested independently against the original finding, and a finding is closed only where the underlying control is demonstrated to operate effectively. The document does not say this was applied at Meridian. [PV-04, Closure Validation Principle]
- Protiviti validates closure through independent evidence testing rather than management assertion, and reports to the accountable committee. [PV-04, Phase 5 — Reporting and Follow-Up]
- PV-04 is a generic risk-based assurance methodology extract (five phases) and describes no specific Meridian engagement. [PV-04, Phase 1 — Planning and Risk Assessment]
- A sanitised credential for a European bank: closure evidence for 41 findings was tested and 9 had been closed without sufficient evidence and needed reopening. This is another client, not Meridian, but is a comparison for the closure validation Meridian deferred. [PV-03, Credential 1 — Financial Crime Remediation Validation, European Bank]
- Sanitised credential for an institutional services group: multi-year co-source across five jurisdictions averaging 22 audits a year. The extract does not name Meridian. [PV-03, Credential 4 — Internal Audit Co-Source, Institutional Services Group]
- The remaining credentials (operational resilience, third-party risk management, AI governance, sanctions screening tuning) are for other clients and none is identified as Meridian work; they match the areas PV-09 shows were discussed with Meridian. [PV-03, Credential 2 — Operational Resilience Implementation, UK Wealth Manager]

### Sources With No Evidence of Prior Protiviti Work

### Recommended Engagement Focus Areas

- Prioritise re-opening the deferred independent closure validation. The Board accepted 'substantially complete' status and raised a risk appetite threshold on management assertion alone, and the Chair asked for formal closure preparation, so the window for independent assurance is closing. The 41-finding/9-reopened credential gives a comparison point. [CL-05, Item 7 — Risk Appetite Review]
- Focus on securing clarity on whether the FY2026 co-source will be extended, expanded to financial crime, or re-tendered. It is the main open commercial question, and the Audit Committee is reviewing co-source sustainability given the expanded FY2026 plan. [PV-09, Open Items at Time of Writing]
- Pursue third-party exit planning, since FY2024 audits found exit planning absent for two of three critical suppliers and the scoped work was not commissioned. [PV-05, Principal Themes Reported]
- Follow up on dependency mapping. The client's internal delivery had left mapping for the four most critical services incomplete at the Board meeting. [CL-05, Item 8 — Operational Resilience]
- Seek earlier access to the second line financial crime team in any expanded scope, since limited access was a recorded lesson of the FY2024 co-source and the largest second-line vacancy gap is in financial crime. [PV-05, Relationship Observations]
- Develop the exploratory AI governance conversation with the CTO, since AI governance is an emerging principal risk named by the Board. [CL-01, Section 3 — Principal Risks and Uncertainties]
