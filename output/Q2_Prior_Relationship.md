# Research Pack: Where has Protiviti worked with Meridian before, and what did we learn?

## 1. Findings

### Prior Protiviti engagements with Meridian: scope, timing and outcome

- In 2023, Protiviti submitted a proposal to Meridian in response to an RFP for internal audit co-source services covering the technology, cyber and financial crime audit disciplines. [PV-01, Client Context]
- The proposal offered specialist audit resource across cyber, cloud, data and financial crime; delivery of an agreed number of audits per annum from the approved internal audit plan; alignment of methodology to the client's existing approach; and quality assurance (QA) and knowledge transfer to the in-house team. [PV-01, Proposed Scope]
- Protiviti was appointed for a two-year term commencing in 2024, covering only a subset of the proposed disciplines. The financial crime audit scope was retained in-house at the client's election. [PV-01, Outcome]
- Internal audit co-source, year one of a two-year term (FY2024): fourteen audits were delivered against a planned fifteen, with one audit deferred at the client's request. [PV-05, Engagement Summary]
- In 2025, Protiviti submitted a proposal to assess cyber security controls across the Meridian Group estate, focused on cloud configuration and identity and access management. [PV-02, Client Context]
- The scope covered cyber control maturity against a recognised framework; cloud security baselines across the UK, Luxembourg and Singapore; privileged access management and joiners/movers/leavers processes; vulnerability management service levels and remediation discipline; and logging and monitoring coverage against the business-critical application inventory. [PV-02, Proposed Scope]
- The proposed team comprised an engagement director, an engagement manager, two cyber specialists and a cloud security specialist, supported by delivery centre resource for evidence analysis. [PV-02, Proposed Team Shape]
- Protiviti was appointed, fieldwork was conducted during 2025, and reporting was issued to the Technology and Operational Resilience Committee. The document does not reproduce the findings or themes reported. [PV-02, Outcome]
- This is a 2025 scoping note/SOW extract, prepared for discussions on support to Meridian beyond the existing internal audit co-source arrangement. [PV-09, Background]
- Independent validation of financial crime remediation closure was under consideration by the client but was deferred pending completion of the programme. [PV-09, Areas Discussed with the Client]
- Support for operational resilience dependency mapping was not taken up; the client indicated that it would deliver this internally in the first instance. [PV-09, Areas Discussed with the Client]
- Third-party exit planning for critical suppliers was scoped but not commissioned, despite PV-05 finding exit planning absent for two of three critical suppliers. [PV-09, Areas Discussed with the Client]
- AI governance framework design was discussed only at an exploratory level with the CTO. [PV-09, Areas Discussed with the Client]

### What Protiviti delivered and what it says about Meridian's control environment

- Named audits delivered were: cloud security configuration (UK and Luxembourg); group-wide identity and access management; vulnerability management; third-party onboarding and monitoring; data protection and cross-border transfers; and change management over phase one of the modernisation programme. A further eight audits were delivered across technology operations and infrastructure. [PV-05, Audits Delivered]

- The sanctions screening tuning audit was deferred at the client's request and carried forward. [PV-05, Deferred Audit]
- Four FY2025 audits were deferred and carried into FY2026: sanctions screening tuning, Hong Kong entity governance, model risk management, and third-party exit planning. [CL-09, Prior Year Carry-Forward]

### Relevant Protiviti methodologies, credentials and thought leadership for reuse

- Protiviti independently validated the closure of findings from a supervisory review for a European bank. It tested closure evidence for 41 findings, found that 9 had been closed without sufficient evidence and required reopening, and delivered a validated closure position to the board and the supervisor. This is reusable for Meridian's remediation-status questions, though it is not Meridian work. [PV-03, Credential 1 — Financial Crime Remediation Validation, European Bank]
- Protiviti ran a multi-year co-source for an Institutional Services Group, covering technology, cyber, cloud and financial crime audits across five jurisdictions and averaging 22 audits per annum. This supports reuse in Meridian co-source discussions, including the financial crime scope that Meridian retained in-house. [PV-03, Credential 4 — Internal Audit Co-Source, Institutional Services Group]
- Other sanitised credentials include operational resilience implementation (UK wealth manager), third-party risk management uplift (fund administrator, 400+ vendors), an AI governance framework (asset manager), and a sanctions screening tuning review (payments institution). None is identified as Meridian work, and the document does not link them to specific Meridian issues. [PV-03, Credential 2 — Operational Resilience Implementation, UK Wealth Manager]
- Protiviti treats closure validation as a distinct assurance activity: closure evidence is tested independently against the original finding, and a finding is regarded as closed only where the underlying control is demonstrated to operate effectively. This is relevant for assessing management assertions such as "remediation substantially complete". [PV-04, Closure Validation Principle]
- Phase 5 reports to the accountable committee and validates the closure of actions through independent evidence testing rather than management assertion. [PV-04, Phase 5 — Reporting and Follow-Up]
- The approach tests design effectiveness (walkthroughs, documentation review and identification of design gaps) and operating effectiveness (risk-based sampling, full-population analytics where data permits, and evidence validation). It rates findings by risk, with root cause and agreed owners and dates. The approach is generic and does not reference Meridian findings. [PV-04, Phase 3 — Operating Effectiveness]
- Protiviti's point of view is that firms pursuing growth in regulated activity often under-invest in control capacity, assuming that existing controls will scale. Control capacity then becomes the binding constraint, and supervisors test whether capability was scaled ahead of volume. [PV-08, The Pattern We See]
- Three failure modes are identified: growth approved on a remediation programme reported as complete without independently validated closure evidence; control resourcing lagging the commercial plan by 12–18 months; and governance forums set up after deployment (notably for AI). These echo Meridian-relevant themes (remediation status and AI governance), though the document does not name Meridian specifically. [PV-08, Three Failure Modes]
- What good looks like: independent validation of remediation closure before stand-down; a control capacity plan tied to the growth plan, with volume triggers; governance as a precondition of deployment; and board reporting that distinguishes management assertion from independently validated position. [PV-08, What Good Looks Like]

## 2. Conflicts and Reconciliation

**Subject:** Status of Meridian's financial crime remediation programme (closed vs. four findings still open)

- **Source A:** The board minutes record Compliance saying the programme is 'substantially complete'. All twelve high-priority findings are said to be remediated and closed with second-line-accepted evidence. Only three medium-priority findings remain open, and no high-priority findings are open. [CL-05, Item 6 — Financial Crime Remediation Programme]
- **Source B:** The press report says four high-priority findings from the 2024 supervisory review remain open. These include beneficial ownership verification for complex fund structures and sanctions screening tuning. It describes this as materially different from the Q3 2025 board reporting. [EX-07, Remediation Status Questioned]
- **Resolution basis:** unresolved
- **Resolution:** The evidence does not let us pick a side. The board minutes are a first-party but self-reported management assertion. The press report's contrary claim, and its claim that some findings were reopened after validation, come from unattributed sources and are unconfirmed. The press report itself says the open-findings claim cannot be verified, because the supervisory correspondence and the board report are not reproduced. The Risk Committee minutes put the supervisor correspondence in a confidential annex that is unavailable. The management statement in the annual report is softer ('substantial progress, some workstreams continuing'), which sits closer to the press view but does not settle the matter. The FY2026 audit plan's planned closure validation would resolve it but has not yet been done. The conflict is therefore flagged as unresolved. It also bears on the Board's decision to raise the Institutional Services onboarding appetite.
