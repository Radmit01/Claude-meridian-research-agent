# Research Pack: What are Meridian Financial Group's principal regulatory and compliance exposures?

## 1. Findings

### Financial Crime, AML and Sanctions Exposure

- The Board identifies financial crime risk, including AML and sanctions compliance, as a principal risk, particularly in Institutional Services where onboarding volumes have grown sharply. [CL-01, Section 3 — Principal Risks and Uncertainties]
- The Q3 2025 Board was told the financial crime remediation programme is 'substantially complete': all twelve high-priority findings were remediated and closed, with closure evidence reviewed and accepted by the second line; three medium-priority findings remain open, and the Group Head of Compliance confirmed no high-priority findings remain open. [CL-05, Item 6 — Financial Crime Remediation Programme]
- A press report states supervisors do not regard the programme as complete; four high-priority findings from the 2024 supervisory review reportedly remain open (including beneficial ownership verification for complex fund structures and sanctions screening tuning), and some findings previously reported as closed were reopened after independent validation testing. The sourcing is anonymous and unconfirmed. This conflicts with CL-05 and is UNRESOLVED: both positions are reported, and the discrepancy should be validated against supervisory records. [EX-07, Remediation Status Questioned]
- Management states substantial progress has been made across the financial crime remediation portfolio, but certain workstreams remain in delivery and will extend into the next financial year; open items are not quantified. [CL-01, Section 5 — Statement on Remediation Activity]
- The regulatory remediation agenda is described to investors as 'well advanced and moving to completion', a management characterisation. [CL-10, Key Messages to Market]
- The new CEO has a mandate that includes completing the regulatory remediation agenda, and named completing the remediation of control weaknesses as an early priority. This is consistent with remediation being incomplete, but does not establish the number or priority of open findings. [EX-06, Press Report — Meridian Appoints New Group Chief Executive]
- Independent validation of financial crime remediation closure was under consideration by the client but deferred pending completion of the programme, so closure had not been independently validated. [PV-09, Areas Discussed with the Client]
- The FY2026 internal audit plan includes financial crime audits covering customer due diligence, enhanced due diligence for complex structures, sanctions screening effectiveness and tuning, transaction monitoring, and closure validation of the remediation programme. [CL-09, Thematic Coverage]
- The Board approved an increase in the onboarding appetite threshold in Institutional Services, explicitly premised on financial crime controls having been remediated and assessed as effective; if that premise is wrong, the exposure is compounded. [CL-05, Item 7 — Risk Appetite Review]
- Payments and sanctions screening runs through a third-party vendor solution (Supplier C), with tuning last reviewed in 2023. [CL-08, Core Platforms]
- The sanctions screening tuning audit was deferred at client request and carried forward, so it remains unaudited in year one. [PV-05, Deferred Audit]
- Four FY2025 audits were deferred into FY2026: sanctions screening tuning, Hong Kong entity governance, model risk management, and third-party exit planning. [CL-09, Prior Year Carry-Forward]
- Second line headcount was 214 against an approved establishment of 247 (a gap of 33); the financial crime function carries the largest vacancy gap with 19 unfilled roles. [CL-03, Second Line Structure]
- Group Head of Compliance Martin Ashby is accountable for the financial crime remediation portfolio. [CL-03, Executive Committee]
- Analysts questioned management on the regulatory position in respect of financial crime controls; the extract does not reproduce management's answer. [CL-10, Analyst Question and Answer Summary]
- Analysts noted that if the discrepancy is confirmed, it would echo a peer-firm enforcement action in which inaccurate internal reporting to the board was an aggravating factor. [EX-07, Remediation Status Questioned]
- Regulatory overhang regarding financial crime is likely to constrain inorganic options and the approval of entity expansion. [EX-08, Implications for Meridian]
- The growth ambition in institutional services is sound in principle but concentrates the Group in the activity attracting the greatest supervisory scrutiny. [EX-08, Implications for Meridian]
- The CEO said growth ambitions in institutional services must be supported by appropriate control capability. [EX-06, Press Report — Meridian Appoints New Group Chief Executive]
- Protiviti's access to the second line financial crime team was limited because that scope was retained in-house, so the audit view of financial crime controls is limited. [PV-05, Relationship Observations]
- The client had not confirmed whether the FY2026 co-source arrangement would be extended, expanded to include financial crime disciplines, or re-tendered, so financial crime assurance coverage for FY2026 is uncertain. [PV-09, Open Items at Time of Writing]

### External Benchmarks: Sector Financial Crime Supervisory Findings (context only, not Meridian-specific)

- Beneficial ownership identification for multi-layered fund structures was inadequate at nine of fourteen firms reviewed; firms relied on client attestation without independent verification beyond the second layer. [EX-02, Principal Findings]
- EDD triggers were inconsistently applied, particularly where a client relationship spanned more than one entity or jurisdiction. [EX-02, Principal Findings]
- Sanctions screening configurations had not been tuned or independently tested within 24 months at eight firms; fuzzy matching thresholds were frequently set without documented rationale. [EX-02, Principal Findings]
- Transaction monitoring rule sets were largely inherited from legacy implementations and not recalibrated for changes in client mix. [EX-02, Principal Findings]
- Financial crime resourcing had not kept pace with onboarding growth at eleven firms; several accepted growth targets without a control capacity plan. [EX-02, Principal Findings]
- Closure of remediation findings was in several cases evidenced by management assertion rather than independent validation. [EX-02, Principal Findings]
- The thematic review does not name Meridian; its relevance depends on matching Meridian's business profile from other documents. [EX-02, Scope]
- Firms that pursue growth in regulated activity before completing control remediation are materially more likely to experience supervisory intervention. This is a generic industry observation and not Meridian-specific. [EX-04, Observation on Sequencing]
- Supervisors are moving toward demonstrable effectiveness testing and expect independent validation of control performance and remediation closure. [EX-09, Financial Crime]

### Operational Resilience and Third-Party Concentration

- Operational resilience risk arises from concentration on a small number of critical third-party technology providers. [CL-01, Section 3 — Principal Risks and Uncertainties]
- The Group has identified its important business services but is still setting impact tolerances; mapping of third-party dependencies for the four most critical services remains incomplete. [CL-05, Item 8 — Operational Resilience]
- Exit plans exist for only one of three critical technology suppliers, and audit rights and incident notification provisions are inconsistent; the Board requested a remediation plan by end of Q1 2026. [CL-06, Item 5 — Third-Party Risk]
- Supplier B (fund administration platform) and Supplier C (payments and screening) have no documented exit plan; only Supplier A (core hosting) has one. [CL-08, Critical Third-Party Suppliers]
- The Supplier C contract does not contain a right-to-audit clause. [CL-08, Critical Third-Party Suppliers]
- The fund administration platform is vendor-hosted by a third party in Luxembourg with no documented exit plan. [CL-08, Core Platforms]
- Exit planning is absent for two of three critical suppliers per the Protiviti audit themes. [PV-05, Principal Themes Reported]
- Third-party exit planning for critical suppliers was scoped but not commissioned. [PV-09, Areas Discussed with the Client]
- Operational resilience dependency mapping support was discussed; the client indicated it would deliver this internally in the first instance. [PV-09, Areas Discussed with the Client]
- A Q4 2025 disruption to client servicing platforms lasted about 31 hours, affecting retail and institutional clients, attributed to a third-party hosting provider failure; the Group said no client data was compromised and the incident was reported to supervisors. [EX-07, Remediation Status Questioned]
- The Group operates 312 applications (47 business critical); the estate is materially fragmented following three acquisitions. Core banking is a legacy on-premise platform scheduled for migration in 2027. [CL-08, Estate Summary]
- Digital operational resilience requirements extend to critical ICT third-party providers, covering contractual minimum provisions, incident reporting timelines, threat-led penetration testing and registers of information. This is generic horizon scanning, not Meridian-specific. [EX-09, Digital Operational Resilience]

### Technology, Cyber and Change Exposure

- Technology change risk is associated with the multi-year core platform modernisation programme. [CL-01, Section 3 — Principal Risks and Uncertainties]
- The Core Platform Modernisation Programme is about four months behind baseline, data platform migration wave one has slipped to Q2 2026, and forecast spend has increased by GBP 14 million. [CL-06, Item 4 — Core Platform Modernisation Programme]

## 2. Conflicts and Reconciliation

**Subject:** Whether the financial crime remediation programme's high-priority findings are all closed

- **Source A:** The Q3 2025 Board was told the programme is 'substantially complete'. All twelve high-priority findings were remediated and closed. Only three medium-priority findings remain open. The Group Head of Compliance confirmed no high-priority findings remain open. [CL-05, Item 6 — Financial Crime Remediation Programme]
- **Source B:** A press report says supervisors do not regard the programme as complete. Four high-priority findings from the 2024 supervisory review reportedly remain open, including beneficial ownership verification and sanctions screening tuning. Some findings previously reported as closed were reportedly reopened after independent validation testing. [EX-07, Remediation Status Questioned]
- **Resolution basis:** unresolved
- **Resolution:** The evidence does not let us pick a side. CL-05 is a management and Board assertion, and the document itself says closure evidence was not independently verified or reproduced. EX-07 is later, but its claim rests on anonymous sources and is unconfirmed. The underlying supervisory correspondence is not reproduced, and the Group declined to comment. Later, indirect sources are consistent with the programme being incomplete: CL-01 (workstreams still in delivery), CL-03, EX-06 (the new CEO's remediation priority) and PV-09 (validation deferred pending completion). CL-09 also schedules closure validation. None of these independently establishes the count or priority of open findings. CL-10's 'well advanced and moving to completion' is a management characterisation. EX-02 and EX-09 only note the sector-wide pattern of closure by assertion. Recency favours EX-07, but weak sourcing prevents resolving on that basis. Both positions should be reported, with the discrepancy flagged for validation against supervisory records.
