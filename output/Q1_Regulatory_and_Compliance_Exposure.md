# Research Pack: What are Meridian Financial Group's principal regulatory and compliance exposures?

## 1. Findings

### Financial Crime Controls and Remediation Status

- The Board was told the financial crime remediation programme is substantially complete: all twelve high-priority findings closed, three medium-priority open, and a request to move to formal closure. This is management's own assertion and is disputed by external reporting (see conflict below). [CL-05, Item 6 — Financial Crime Remediation Programme]
- External press reporting, citing anonymous sources, says supervisors do not regard remediation as complete: four high-priority findings remain open (including beneficial ownership verification and sanctions screening tuning), and some findings previously reported closed were reopened after independent testing found closure evidence insufficient. The conflict with CL-05 is UNRESOLVED: CL-05 is a primary document but records only management assertion; EX-07 is second-hand and the Group declined to comment. [EX-07, Remediation Status Questioned]
- Management states that certain financial crime workstreams remain in delivery and will extend into the next financial year, which is inconsistent with a 'substantially complete' framing but is not a direct count of open findings. [CL-01, Section 5 — Statement on Remediation Activity]
- The Board raised the Institutional Services onboarding volume appetite threshold on the premise that financial crime controls were remediated and effective, so the Group is exposed if the remediation claim is overstated. [CL-05, Item 7 — Risk Appetite Review]
- Payments and sanctions screening runs on a third-party vendor solution whose tuning was last reviewed in 2023. The sanctions screening tuning audit was deferred at client request in FY2024 and again carried into FY2026. [CL-08, Core Platforms]
- Four FY2025 audits were deferred into FY2026: sanctions screening tuning, Hong Kong entity governance, model risk management and third-party exit planning. These fall in areas of supervisory focus, leaving assurance gaps. [CL-09, Prior Year Carry-Forward]
- Independent validation of financial crime remediation closure was deferred by the client pending completion of the programme, which implies the programme was still incomplete in 2025. [PV-09, Areas Discussed with the Client]
- The FY2026 internal audit plan covers CDD, EDD for complex structures, sanctions screening effectiveness and tuning, transaction monitoring, and closure validation of the remediation programme. [CL-09, Thematic Coverage]

### Supervisory Expectations and Peer Enforcement Read-Across

- Supervisors expect CDD, EDD for complex ownership structures, sanctions screening and transaction monitoring to be effective in practice, with control capability scaled ahead of growth into higher-risk segments. [EX-01, Priority Area One — Financial Crime Controls]
- Beneficial ownership identification for multi-layered fund structures was inadequate at nine of fourteen firms reviewed. Sanctions screening was untuned or untested within 24 months at eight firms. Financial crime resourcing lagged onboarding growth at eleven firms. [EX-02, Principal Findings]
- Closure of remediation findings was in several cases evidenced by management assertion rather than independent validation. [EX-02, Principal Findings]
- A comparable mid-size institutional services provider was penalised and required to appoint an independent skilled person over financial crime failings spanning four years. Supervisors have written to sector firms about remediation closure assurance and growth-versus-control alignment. [EX-03, Summary]
- At the peer firm the board was told remediation was complete, but independent testing found findings closed without adequate evidence. The supervisor called inaccurate board reporting the most serious aggravating factor, as it delayed corrective action by about 18 months. [EX-03, Regulatory Commentary]
- The themes supervisors flagged match those arising at Meridian, although the document does not name Meridian. [EX-03, Read-Across]
- Firms that pursue growth in regulated activity before completing control remediation are materially more likely to face supervisory intervention. [EX-04, Observation on Sequencing]
- Supervisors are moving toward demonstrable effectiveness testing and expect independent validation of control performance and of remediation closure. [EX-09, Financial Crime]
- An analyst view is that regulatory overhang on financial crime is likely to constrain Meridian's inorganic options and entity expansion approvals, and that Institutional Services growth concentrates the Group in the most scrutinised activity. [EX-08, Implications for Meridian]
- The press report says Q3 2025 board reporting described remediation as substantially complete, while a person close to the supervisor said some closed findings were reopened. The Group declined to comment. Analysts noted the parallel to the peer enforcement action. [EX-07, Remediation Status Questioned]

### Operational Resilience and Third-Party Risk

- Important business services are identified and impact tolerances are being set, but third-party dependency mapping for the four most critical services is incomplete. [CL-05, Item 8 — Operational Resilience]
- Incomplete dependency mapping is the most commonly incomplete element of operational resilience and is treated as a material shortcoming. [EX-01, Priority Area Two — Operational Resilience]
- In Q4 2025 Meridian had a roughly 31-hour disruption to client servicing platforms attributed to a third-party hosting provider failure. The Group said no client data was compromised and the incident was reported to supervisors. [EX-07, Remediation Status Questioned]
- Dependence on three critical technology suppliers, with exit plans for only one. Supplier B has no documented exit plan. Supplier C has no exit plan and no right-to-audit clause. [CL-08, Critical Third-Party Suppliers]
- Audit-rights and incident-notification provisions are inconsistent across the supplier estate. The Board requested a remediation plan by end of Q1 2026. [CL-06, Item 5 — Third-Party Risk]
- Supervisors expect a complete outsourcing register, documented exit plans for all critical suppliers, contractual audit rights and a concentration risk assessment. [EX-01, Priority Area Three — Third-Party and Outsourcing Risk]
- Protiviti's year-one audits found third-party exit planning absent for two of three critical suppliers. [PV-05, Principal Themes Reported]
- Requirements are extending to critical ICT providers, including contractual minimum provisions, incident reporting timelines, threat-led penetration testing and a register of information on all third-party arrangements. [EX-09, Digital Operational Resilience]

### Technology, Cyber, Data and Privacy

- About 34% of workloads are cloud-hosted. Security baselines are not consistently applied across regional entities, and IAM is federated in the UK but separate in Singapore and Hong Kong. [CL-08, Cloud Posture]
- Privileged access review coverage is incomplete in the Asian entities, vulnerability remediation SLAs were missed in each of the last four quarters, and logging and monitoring does not cover all business-critical applications. [CL-08, Known Technology Control Gaps]
- Protiviti reported inconsistent security baselines (Asian entities materially behind), repeated missed vulnerability service levels, and fragmented IAM with no single view of privileged access. [PV-05, Principal Themes Reported]
- Supervisors expect multi-jurisdiction firms to evidence lawful transfer mechanisms, defined retention schedules and control over data in cloud environments. The FY2026 audit plan covers cross-border transfers, retention and regulatory reporting data quality. [EX-01, Priority Area Five — Data and Cross-Border Transfers]
- The core platform programme is about four months behind baseline, data platform migration wave one slipped to Q2 2026, and forecast spend is up GBP 14 million. [CL-06, Item 4 — Core Platform Modernisation Programme]
- Assurance over the modernisation programme found optimistic milestone reporting and insufficient independent challenge of status. [PV-05, Principal Themes Reported]
- There are 312 applications (47 business critical), fragmented by three acquisitions. Core banking is a legacy on-premise platform scheduled for migration in 2027. [CL-08, Estate Summary]

### AI Governance

- The AI governance forum has not been constituted, and the FY2025 effectiveness review found no defined AI governance framework. [CL-07, Governance Structure Beneath the Committee]
- There is no consolidated inventory of models or AI-enabled tooling across the Group. [CL-08, Known Technology Control Gaps]
- The AI governance readiness audit is deferred pending establishment of the framework. [CL-09, Thematic Coverage]
- Firms must keep an AI inventory, apply proportionate governance and model risk controls, evidence human oversight, and not scale deployment ahead of governance. [EX-01, Priority Area Six — Artificial Intelligence]
- AI was not a discrete principal risk category in FY2024 and conduct was only emerging, so these exposures have escalated in FY2025. [CL-02, Section 3 — Principal Risks]

### Conduct, Consumer Outcomes and Redress

- A GBP 31 million provision has been recognised for the Retail Banking legacy product governance review. Affected customer review is ongoing and the final redress population is not yet determined. [CL-06, Item 8 — Consumer Outcomes]
- Profit before tax fell to GBP 214m from GBP 241m, attributed mainly to remediation costs, technology investment and the GBP 31m redress provision. Cost-to-income rose to 71.2% and CET1 fell to 14.1%. [CL-01, Section 2 — Financial Highlights]
- Firms must evidence good customer outcomes across the product lifecycle, including legacy products no longer actively sold. Redress is often revised upwards sector-wide. [EX-01, Priority Area Four — Consumer Outcomes]

### Governance, Leadership and Assurance Capacity

- There is no permanent Group Chief Risk Officer. The Deputy CRO attends Committee meetings, and the departure creates a leadership transition exposure during remediation. [CL-07, Composition and Meetings]
- The FY2025 effectiveness review identified inconsistent regional risk reporting, limited aggregation of third-party risk data and no defined AI governance framework. [CL-07, Known Gaps]
- The 38-audit FY2026 plan needs about 6,200 days against internal capacity of 4,900. The roughly 1,300-day shortfall is to be met by co-source in cyber, cloud, financial crime and resilience. [CL-09, Resourcing]
- The client had not confirmed whether the co-source arrangement would be extended, expanded to cover financial crime disciplines, or re-tendered. The existing arrangement does not cover financial crime. [PV-09, Open Items at Time of Writing]
- Management says the pace of regulatory change is a material demand on second and third line capacity. [CL-01, Section 4 — Regulatory Environment]

### Recommended Engagement Focus Areas

- Concentrate on independent validation of financial crime remediation closure. The central conflict between the board's view and the press report is unresolved, and closure by management assertion is the specific weakness supervisors and the peer enforcement case treated as aggravating. Independent validation would resolve the dispute directly. [EX-03, Regulatory Commentary]
- Pursue extension of the co-source scope to financial crime disciplines. Protiviti's view of financial crime controls is limited because that scope was held in-house, the existing co-source does not cover financial crime, and the client has not decided the FY2026 arrangement. [PV-09, Open Items at Time of Writing]
- Prioritise an independent sanctions screening tuning and effectiveness review. Tuning was last reviewed in 2023, the audit has been deferred twice, and the peer enforcement case cited three years without independent tuning review. [CL-08, Core Platforms]
- Support the Board and Audit Committee in testing the quality of closure evidence before the risk appetite increase in Institutional Services is relied on, since that increase is premised on controls being remediated. [CL-05, Item 7 — Risk Appetite Review]
- Offer support on exit planning for the two suppliers without plans and on audit-rights gaps. Exit planning was scoped but not commissioned, the item was deferred in FY2025, and the Board has asked for a remediation plan by end of Q1 2026. [PV-09, Areas Discussed with the Client]
- Offer dependency mapping support for the four most critical services. The client said it would deliver this internally first, the Board is still awaiting an update, and supervisors treat incomplete mapping as a material shortcoming. [CL-05, Item 8 — Operational Resilience]
- Position early framework design support: no framework, forum or model inventory exists, the AI audit is deferred pending a framework, and supervisors expect inventory and governance before AI deployment scales. [CL-08, Known Technology Control Gaps]
- Focus technology assurance on the Asian entities, where privileged access coverage is incomplete, security baselines are materially behind and IAM is separate. [PV-05, Principal Themes Reported]
- Monitor and support scoping of the legacy product redress population, which remains undetermined, since redress is often revised upwards sector-wide. [CL-06, Item 8 — Consumer Outcomes]

## 2. Conflicts and Reconciliation

**Subject:** Status of Meridian's financial crime remediation programme: how many findings remain open, and at what priority

- **Source A:** The board was told the programme is substantially complete. All twelve high-priority findings from the 2024 supervisory review are remediated and closed, and no high-priority findings are open. Three medium-priority findings remain open and are due to close in the current quarter. [CL-05, Item 6 — Financial Crime Remediation Programme]
- **Source B:** Persons familiar with the supervisory dialogue say supervisors do not regard remediation as complete. Four high-priority findings remain open, including beneficial ownership verification and sanctions screening tuning. Some findings previously reported as closed were reopened after independent validation testing found the closure evidence insufficient. [EX-07, Remediation Status Questioned]
- **Resolution basis:** unresolved
- **Resolution:** The evidence does not let us pick a side. CL-05 is a primary document, but it records management's own assertion of closure, and EX-02, EX-03 and EX-09 show that closure by management assertion is a known weakness. EX-07 is a press report that relies on anonymous sources and does not reproduce the supervisory correspondence, so it is second-hand. The Group declined to comment. CL-01 (management says some workstreams extend into next year) and PV-09 (independent validation deferred until the programme is complete) point towards incompleteness. Neither is a direct count of open high-priority findings, so they do not settle the dispute. Recency does not help either. CL-05 reflects the Q3 2025 board position and EX-07 is a later report, but EX-07's claim about the board position is itself unverified. The discrepancy should be reported as open, and the supervisory correspondence or an independent closure validation would be needed to resolve it.
