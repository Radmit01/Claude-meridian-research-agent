### Protiviti Point of View on Growth and Control Capacity

- Firms pursuing growth in regulated activity often under-invest in control capacity on the assumption existing controls will scale; control capacity is the binding constraint and supervisors increasingly test whether capability was scaled ahead of volume. [PV-08, The Pattern We See]
- A failure mode is growth approved on the basis of a remediation programme reported as complete, where closure evidence has not been independently validated. This is relevant to the Meridian CL-05 'substantially complete' vs EX-07 'four findings remain open' conflict. [PV-08, Three Failure Modes]
- A failure mode is control resourcing plans that lag the commercial plan by twelve to eighteen months. [PV-08, Three Failure Modes]
- A failure mode is governance forums constituted after deployment rather than before, most visibly in AI adoption. [PV-08, Three Failure Modes]
- Good practice is independent validation of remediation closure before the programme is stood down. [PV-08, What Good Looks Like]
- Good practice is an explicit control capacity plan linked to the commercial growth plan, with volume triggers. [PV-08, What Good Looks Like]
- Good practice is governance established as a precondition of deployment, not a follow-on workstream. [PV-08, What Good Looks Like]
- Good practice is board reporting that distinguishes management assertion from independently validated position. [PV-08, What Good Looks Like]
