# Research Pack: Produce the full pre-engagement client research pack for Meridian Financial Group.

## 1. Findings

### Client Profile and Strategy

- Meridian Financial Group plc is a mid-size financial services group headquartered in London, with operations in the UK, Ireland, Luxembourg, Singapore and Hong Kong. It has about 4,800 employees and GBP 91.4bn assets under administration at 31 Dec 2025. [CL-01, Section 1 — Group Overview]
- Three segments: Wealth & Asset Management 46% of revenue, Retail Banking 34%, Institutional Services 20%. In FY2024 the mix was 49%, 33% and 18%, so Institutional Services is growing in share. [CL-01, Section 1 — Group Overview]
- Meridian aims to be the leading mid-size wealth and institutional services provider in its chosen markets, on a single modern technology estate with a materially lower cost-to-income ratio. [CL-04, Strategic Ambition]
- The target is to double fund administration and depositary revenue by 2028, focused on Luxembourg and Singapore. Growth is expected to raise onboarding volumes of complex corporate and fund structures by about 140 per cent. [CL-04, Four Strategic Pillars]
- The plan is to complete cloud migration of core banking, client servicing and data platforms by end 2028 and decommission fourteen legacy applications. [CL-04, Four Strategic Pillars]
- The strategy depends on three enablers: completing financial crime remediation, a group-wide operational resilience capability meeting supervisory expectations, and recruitment into second line vacancies. Failure to complete remediation could constrain regulatory approval for Luxembourg and Singapore expansion. [CL-04, Dependencies and Enablers]
- Alanna Reyes became Group CEO in Feb 2026. Her stated priorities are completing remediation of control weaknesses, stabilising the technology modernisation programme, and ensuring Institutional Services growth is supported by appropriate control capability. [EX-06, Press Report — Meridian Appoints New Group Chief Executive]

### Financial Performance and Peer Position

- FY2025 total income was GBP 1,284m (FY2024: 1,196m). Profit before tax fell to GBP 214m (FY2024: 241m). Cost-to-income rose to 71.2% (67.8%) and CET1 fell to 14.1% (14.9%). [CL-01, Section 2 — Financial Highlights]
- The profit decline is attributed mainly to elevated regulatory remediation costs, technology investment, and a GBP 31m legacy customer redress provision in Retail Banking. [CL-01, Section 2 — Financial Highlights]
- Cost-to-income of 71.2% compares with a peer median of 66.4%. CET1 of 14.1% compares with a peer median of 15.2%. Regulatory and remediation spend is 4.8% of income against a peer median of 2.9%. [EX-05, Peer Benchmarking]
- Management guided to elevated regulatory and remediation costs continuing through FY2026, moderating thereafter. Analysts questioned whether the 62% cost-to-income target by 2028 is sustainable given programme delays. [CL-10, Guidance]

### Financial Crime Remediation (Conflict)

- The Board was told the programme is substantially complete. All twelve high-priority findings were reported closed, with closure evidence accepted by the second line. Three medium-priority findings remain open. [CL-05, Item 6 — Financial Crime Remediation Programme]
- Anonymous sources say four high-priority findings from the 2024 supervisory review remain open, including beneficial ownership verification and sanctions screening tuning. They also say some findings reported as closed were reopened after independent validation testing. [EX-07, Remediation Status Questioned]
- UNRESOLVED. CL-05 is management's own assertion, accepted by the second line rather than independently validated. EX-07 relies on unverifiable anonymous sources. The two may also describe different points in time. Independent closure validation is needed to settle the question. Softer statements in CL-01 and CL-10 do not claim completion. [CL-05, Item 6 — Financial Crime Remediation Programme]
- CL-01 says substantial progress has been made but certain workstreams will extend into the next financial year. This is not a claim of completion. [CL-01, Section 5 — Statement on Remediation Activity]
- Investors were told remediation is 'well advanced and moving to completion'. [CL-10, Key Messages to Market]
- The Board raised the Institutional Services onboarding-volume appetite threshold on the basis that financial crime controls had been remediated and assessed as effective. That basis is the disputed claim. [CL-05, Item 7 — Risk Appetite Review]
- A peer reported remediation as complete to its board, but independent testing later found findings closed without adequate evidence. The supervisor cited inaccurate board reporting as the most serious aggravating factor, which delayed corrective action by about eighteen months. Supervisors have asked sector firms to consider read-across. [EX-03, Regulatory Commentary]
- In several firms, closure of remediation findings was evidenced by management assertion rather than independent validation. [EX-02, Principal Findings]

### Financial Crime Controls and Growth Alignment

- Firms must show that customer due diligence, enhanced due diligence, sanctions screening and transaction monitoring are effective in practice. Firms expanding into higher-risk segments are expected to scale control capability ahead of growth. [EX-01, Priority Area One — Financial Crime Controls]
- At fourteen firms, beneficial ownership identification was inadequate at nine, sanctions screening was untuned for 24 months at eight, and resourcing lagged onboarding growth at eleven. [EX-02, Principal Findings]
- Meridian's payments and sanctions screening runs on a third-party vendor solution whose tuning was last reviewed in 2023. [CL-08, Core Platforms]
- Second line headcount is 214 against an establishment of 247. Financial crime has the largest gap, with 19 unfilled roles. [CL-03, Second Line Structure]
- Meridian's financial crime vacancy rate is 19% against a peer median of 11% and an upper quartile of 6%. Second line headcount is 4.5% of total against a peer median of 6.1%. [EX-05, Peer Benchmarking]
- Firms pursuing growth in regulated activity before completing remediation are materially more likely to see supervisory intervention. [EX-04, Observation on Sequencing]
- Financial crime overhang is likely to constrain inorganic options and approval of entity expansion. Institutional Services growth concentrates the Group in the most scrutinised activity. [EX-08, Implications for Meridian]
- A comparable mid-size institutional services provider faced enforcement over a four-year period. It was fined and required to appoint an independent skilled person after expanding fund services without commensurate financial crime resourcing. [EX-03, Nature of the Failings]

### Governance, Leadership and Risk Oversight

- The Group CRO role has been vacant since Nov 2025. Deputy CRO Hannah Okafor provides interim cover, and the Board agreed an external search. [CL-03, Executive Committee]
- Analysts regard the lack of a permanent CRO alongside the scale of the regulatory agenda as an execution risk. [EX-06, Press Report — Meridian Appoints New Group Chief Executive]
- Board-identified principal risks are financial crime, operational resilience, conduct in Retail Banking, cloud data protection, technology change and emerging AI risk. [CL-01, Section 3 — Principal Risks and Uncertainties]
- The FY2025 effectiveness review found inconsistent regional risk reporting, limited aggregation of third-party risk data, and no AI governance framework. [CL-07, Known Gaps]
- Internal Audit has 41 practitioners. The Audit Committee has asked management to review the sustainability of the co-source arrangement given the expanded FY2026 plan. [CL-03, Third Line Structure]
- The FY2026 plan of 38 audits needs about 6,200 days against internal capacity of 4,900, a shortfall of about 1,300 days that co-source is expected to cover. [CL-09, Resourcing]
- Four FY2025 audits were deferred into FY2026: sanctions screening tuning, Hong Kong entity governance, model risk management, and third-party exit planning. [CL-09, Prior Year Carry-Forward]

### Operational Resilience and Third-Party Risk

- Important business services are identified and impact tolerances are being set, but third-party dependency mapping for the four most critical services is incomplete. [CL-05, Item 8 — Operational Resilience]
- Only 40% of important business services have completed dependency mapping, against a peer median of 78%. Incomplete mapping is regarded by supervisors as a material shortcoming. [EX-05, Peer Benchmarking]
- Supplier A (core hosting) has an exit plan. Supplier B (fund administration) has none. Supplier C (payments and screening) has no exit plan and no right-to-audit clause. [CL-08, Critical Third-Party Suppliers]
- Protiviti's FY2024 co-source found exit planning absent for two of three critical suppliers. [PV-05, [PV-05, Principal Themes Reported]]
- A disruption lasting about thirty-one hours, attributed to a third-party hosting provider failure, affected online access for retail and institutional clients. It was reported to supervisors. [EX-07, Remediation Status Questioned]
- Supervisors expect documented exit plans for all critical suppliers, contractual audit rights and a concentration risk assessment. [EX-01, Priority Area Three — Third-Party and Outsourcing Risk]

### Technology Modernisation, Cyber and Cloud

- The core platform modernisation programme is about four months behind baseline. Data platform wave one has slipped to Q2 2026, and forecast spend is up GBP 14m. [CL-06, Item 4 — Core Platform Modernisation Programme]
- RESOLVED on authority/independence grounds. CL-06 (specific delays and cost increase) is preferred over the CL-10 'on track' investor message. PV-05, EX-08 and CL-08 corroborate the slippage. The 2028 target may still be recoverable. [CL-06, Item 4 — Core Platform Modernisation Programme]
- Protiviti's assurance found optimistic milestone reporting and insufficient independent challenge of programme status. [PV-05, [PV-05, Principal Themes Reported]]
- The Group runs 312 applications, 47 of them business critical, with duplicate core capability following three acquisitions. [CL-08, Estate Summary]
- About 34% of workloads are cloud-hosted, against a peer median of 52%. Security baselines are not consistently applied across regions, and IAM is separate in Singapore and Hong Kong. [CL-08, Cloud Posture]
- Privileged access reviews are incomplete in the Asian entities, vulnerability remediation service levels were missed in each of the last four quarters, and logging does not cover all business-critical applications. [CL-08, Known Technology Control Gaps]

### Conduct and Consumer Outcomes

- A GBP 31m provision is recognised for the Retail Banking legacy product governance review. The affected customer population is still under review and the final redress population is undetermined. [CL-06, Item 8 — Consumer Outcomes]
- Management said a further provision cannot be ruled out. [CL-10, Analyst Question and Answer Summary]
- Firms must evidence good outcomes across the product lifecycle, including legacy products no longer sold. [EX-01, Priority Area Four — Consumer Outcomes]

### AI Governance and Emerging Regulation

- Meridian has no approved AI governance framework, AI inventory or generative AI model risk controls. These are prerequisites to deployment beyond pilot scale. [CL-04, Artificial Intelligence Ambition]
- The AI governance forum referenced in Group strategy has not been constituted. The Risk Committee nonetheless has oversight of AI adoption. [CL-07, Governance Structure Beneath the Committee]
- 0% of Meridian has an approved AI governance framework, against a peer median of 45%. [EX-05, Peer Benchmarking]
- The AI governance readiness review is deferred pending establishment of the framework. [CL-09, Thematic Coverage]
- Supervisors indicated firms should not scale AI deployment ahead of governance. [EX-01, Priority Area Six — Artificial Intelligence]

### Protiviti Relationship, Capability and White Space

- Protiviti was appointed in 2024 for a two-year internal audit co-source covering a subset of disciplines. Meridian retained financial crime audit in-house at its own election. In FY2024 Protiviti delivered 14 of 15 planned audits. [PV-01, Outcome]
- Protiviti was appointed for a 2025 cyber controls assessment, with reporting issued to the Technology and Operational Resilience Committee. [PV-02, Outcome]
- Engagement was strongest with the Chief Internal Auditor and the CTO. Access to the second-line financial crime team was limited. Scheduling was disrupted twice by client resource availability. [PV-05, [PV-05, Relationship Observations]]
- Protiviti independently validated closure for a European bank. It tested 41 findings, found 9 closed without sufficient evidence, and delivered a validated position to the board and supervisor. [PV-03, [PV-03, Credential 1 — Financial Crime Remediation Validation, European Bank]]
- Protiviti treats closure validation as a distinct assurance activity. A finding counts as closed only where the control is shown to operate effectively, which contrasts with reliance on a board-minute assertion. [PV-04, [PV-04, Closure Validation Principle]]
- Independent closure validation was considered by the client but deferred pending programme completion. Third-party exit planning was scoped but not commissioned. Resilience dependency mapping was to be delivered internally first. AI governance was discussed exploratorily with the CTO. [PV-09, Areas Discussed with the Client]
- The client had not confirmed whether the FY2026 co-source would be extended, expanded to include financial crime, or re-tendered. Additional scope would be a separate SOW under the existing MSA. [PV-09, Open Items at Time of Writing]
- Protiviti's catalogue covers financial crime closure validation and sanctions tuning review, third-party exit planning, operational resilience, AI governance framework design, and programme assurance. [PV-07, Risk and Compliance]
- Firms in Meridian's position typically increase spend on external assurance and specialist advisory support during the remediation and validation phase. [EX-08, View]
- Three failure modes: growth approved on remediation reported complete without independent closure evidence, control resourcing lagging the commercial plan by 12-18 months, and governance forums constituted after deployment. [PV-08, Three Failure Modes]

### Recommended Engagement Focus Areas

The CL-05 vs EX-07 conflict cannot be resolved from the corpus, and only independent testing can establish the true position. Board reporting accuracy is a supervisory aggravating factor in the peer case. Pair this with a control capacity plan linked to the 140% onboarding growth forecast. [CL-05, Item 7 — Risk Appetite Review]

Suppliers B and C have no exit plans, Supplier C has no audit rights, only 40% of services are mapped, and exit planning was scoped but not commissioned. ## 2. Conflicts and Reconciliation

**Subject:** Status of the financial crime remediation programme (closed vs. open findings)

- **Source A:** The Board was told the programme is substantially complete. All twelve high-priority findings are remediated and closed with closure evidence accepted by the second line. Three medium-priority findings remain open. No high-priority findings are open. [CL-05, Item 6 — Financial Crime Remediation Programme]
- **Source B:** Unnamed sources say supervisors do not regard the programme as complete. Four high-priority findings from the 2024 supervisory review reportedly remain open, including beneficial ownership verification and sanctions screening tuning. Some findings previously reported as closed were reopened after independent validation testing. [EX-07, Remediation Status Questioned]
- **Resolution basis:** unresolved
- **Resolution:** Neither source can be preferred. CL-05 is a primary internal record, but it reports management's own assertion, and closure evidence was accepted by the second line, not independently validated. EX-07 is press reporting that relies on anonymous sources, and it cannot be verified. The two documents also describe different points in time, because EX-07 refers to Q3 2025 board reporting and the CL-05 meeting date is not given. EX-02, EX-03 and PV-04 show that management-asserted closure is often unreliable. That is context only, and it does not show which account is correct here. CL-01 (soft 'substantial progress, workstreams continuing') and CL-10 ('well advanced and moving to completion') are softer statements that do not claim completion. Independent closure validation is needed to settle the question.

**Subject:** Whether the technology modernisation programme is on track

- **Source A:** Technology modernisation is stated to be on track to deliver the 62% cost-to-income target by 2028. [CL-10, Key Messages to Market]
- **Source B:** The programme is about four months behind baseline. Data platform migration wave one has slipped to Q2 2026, and forecast spend has risen by GBP 14m. [CL-06, Item 4 — Core Platform Modernisation Programme]
- **Resolution basis:** authority/independence
- **Resolution:** CL-06 is an internal board record that sets out specific delays and cost increases. Independent sources support it: PV-05 found optimistic milestone reporting and insufficient independent challenge, EX-08 notes the programme is 'already reporting slippage', and CL-08 confirms the Q2 2026 slip. CL-10 is investor messaging and gives no supporting detail. The 'on track' claim is therefore given less weight. The two statements may partly differ in scope, because the delay could still be recoverable by 2028.
