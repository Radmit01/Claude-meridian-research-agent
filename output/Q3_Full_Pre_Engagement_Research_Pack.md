# Research Pack: Produce the full pre-engagement client research pack for Meridian Financial Group.

## 1. Findings

### Business profile, financial position and strategic ambitions

- Meridian is a mid-size, London-headquartered financial group operating in the UK, Ireland, Luxembourg, Singapore and Hong Kong. It has approximately 4,800 employees and GBP 91.4bn of assets under administration at 31 December 2025. [CL-01, Section 1 — Group Overview]
- Meridian operates three segments: Wealth & Asset Management (46% of revenue), Retail Banking (34%) and Institutional Services (20%). [CL-01, Section 1 — Group Overview]
- In FY2024, assets under administration were GBP 83.7bn, and the Group employed approximately 4,400 people across five jurisdictions. The segment mix was Wealth & Asset Management 49%, Retail Banking 33% and Institutional Services 18%. [CL-02, Section 1 — Group Overview]
- FY2025 total income was GBP 1,284m (FY2024: GBP 1,196m). Profit before tax was GBP 214m (GBP 241m). The cost-to-income ratio was 71.2% (67.8%), CET1 was 14.1% (14.9%), and customer accounts were 1.9m (1.7m). [CL-01, Section 2 — Financial Highlights]
- The FY2024 baseline was total income of GBP 1,196m, profit before tax of GBP 241m, a cost-to-income ratio of 67.8%, CET1 of 14.9% and 1.7m customer accounts. [CL-02, Section 2 — Financial Highlights]
- The decline in profit before tax is attributed mainly to elevated regulatory remediation costs, technology investment and a GBP 31m legacy customer redress provision in Retail Banking. [CL-01, Section 2 — Financial Highlights]
- Meridian has announced a multi-year modernisation of its core banking and client servicing platforms, with an indicative investment of GBP 180m over four years. It also plans to expand Institutional Services through fund administration and depositary services in Luxembourg and Singapore. [CL-02, Section 4 — Strategic Commentary]
- Meridian's ambition is to become the leading mid-size wealth and institutional services provider, operating on a single modern technology estate with a materially lower cost-to-income ratio. [CL-04, Strategic Ambition]
- Meridian's strategy rests on four pillars. (1) Grow Institutional Services: double fund administration and depositary revenue by 2028, with onboarding volumes of complex structures up approximately 140%. (2) Modernise technology: complete cloud migration by end 2028 and decommission 14 legacy applications. (3) Improve efficiency: reduce the cost-to-income ratio to 62% by 2028 and move to two operating hubs. (4) Embed responsible AI. [CL-04, Four Strategic Pillars]
- The technology roadmap runs as follows. In 2026: cloud landing zone, data platform migration wave one, and onboarding workflow replacement. In 2027: core banking migration waves one and two, and decommissioning of six legacy applications. In 2028: full migration, legacy retirement, and AI at scale. [CL-04, Technology Roadmap Summary]
- The strategy depends on completing financial crime remediation, building a group-wide operational resilience capability, and recruiting into second line vacancies. Failure on remediation would constrain regulatory approval for the Luxembourg and Singapore expansion. [CL-04, Dependencies and Enablers]
- Meridian presents itself as a focused mid-size wealth and institutional services group. It sees a path to margin expansion through technology simplification and operational efficiency. [CL-10, Equity Story]
- Institutional Services is positioned as the growth engine, with a target of doubling revenue by 2028. Technology modernisation is said to be on track to deliver a 62% cost-to-income ratio by 2028. [CL-10, Key Messages to Market]
- Management has guided that elevated regulatory and remediation costs will continue through FY2026 and moderate thereafter. [CL-10, Guidance]
- The Institutional Services growth target, which draws on complex structures, sits alongside control weaknesses and a regulator expectation that control capability be scaled ahead of growth. The revenue-doubling target, together with the technology delay (data platform wave one is delayed), puts the 2028 cost-to-income target at risk. [CL-10, Key Messages to Market]
- The Group runs 312 applications, of which 47 are business critical. The estate is materially fragmented following three historic acquisitions, with duplicate core capability in client servicing and payments. [CL-08, Estate Summary]
- Core banking runs on a legacy, on-premise, vendor-supported platform scheduled for migration in 2027. Client servicing/CRM runs as two parallel platforms across the UK and Luxembourg entities. Fund administration uses a vendor platform hosted by a third party in Luxembourg. The data platform cloud migration is in progress, with wave one delayed to Q2 2026. [CL-08, Core Platforms]
- The 2028 efficiency target depends on sustained delivery of a technology programme that is already reporting slippage. [EX-08, Implications for Meridian]
- The Institutional Services growth ambition is sound in principle, but it concentrates the Group in the activity that attracts the greatest supervisory scrutiny. [EX-08, Implications for Meridian]
- Mid-size financial firms face a structural squeeze between rising compliance costs and subscale distribution. Continued consolidation is expected over the next 36 months. [EX-08, Thesis]
- Meridian's cost-to-income ratio deteriorated year on year and is above the peer median. [EX-08, Implications for Meridian]

### AI ambition and governance readiness

- Meridian plans to deploy AI in onboarding document triage, adviser knowledge assistance, and internal audit and risk analytics. It has no approved AI governance framework, AI inventory or generative-AI model risk controls, all of which are prerequisites beyond pilot scale. [CL-04, Artificial Intelligence Ambition]
- There is no consolidated inventory of models or AI-enabled tooling across the Group. EX-01 expects firms to keep an AI inventory. [CL-08, Known Technology Control Gaps]
- The AI governance readiness review has been deferred pending establishment of the framework. Given regulator expectations and the absence of an AI inventory, this represents a governance gap. [CL-09, Thematic Coverage]
- Meridian has no approved AI governance framework (0%), compared with a peer median of 45% and an upper quartile of 100%. [EX-05, Peer Benchmarking]
- Firms deploying AI, including generative AI, are expected to keep an AI inventory, apply proportionate governance and model risk controls, evidence human oversight of material decisions, and assess consumer and conduct implications. Supervisors state that firms should not scale deployment ahead of establishing governance. [EX-01, Priority Area Six — Artificial Intelligence]
- Emerging AI requirements converge on AI inventory, use-case risk classification, human oversight of consequential decisions, transparency, bias and drift testing, and senior management accountability. AI used in onboarding, credit or advice attracts the highest expectations. [EX-09, Artificial Intelligence]

At 11 firms, financial crime resourcing lagged onboarding growth, and several firms accepted growth targets without a control capacity plan. [EX-02, Principal Findings]

[EX-03, Nature of the Failings]
