# Research Pack: Produce the full pre-engagement client research pack for Meridian Financial Group.

## 1. Findings

### Client Profile and Financial Position

- Meridian Financial Group plc is a London-headquartered financial services group with operations in the UK, Ireland, Luxembourg, Singapore and Hong Kong. It has about 4,800 employees and GBP 91.4bn of assets under administration at 31 Dec 2025 (GBP 83.7bn and about 4,400 employees a year earlier). [CL-01, Section 1 — Group Overview]
- FY2025 revenue split: Wealth & Asset Management 46%, Retail Banking 34%, Institutional Services 20%. In FY2024 Institutional Services was 18%. [CL-01, Section 1 — Group Overview]
- Total income rose to GBP 1,284m (FY2024: 1,196m), but profit before tax fell to GBP 214m (from 241m). Cost-to-income rose to 71.2% (from 67.8%) and CET1 fell to 14.1% (from 14.9%). The decline is attributed to remediation costs, technology investment and a GBP 31m legacy redress provision. [CL-01, Section 2 — Financial Highlights]
- Cost-to-income of 71.2% compares with a peer median of 66.4%. CET1 of 14.1% compares with a peer median of 15.2%. Regulatory and remediation spend is 4.8% of income against a peer median of 2.9%. [EX-05, Peer Benchmarking]
- Management guided that elevated regulatory and remediation costs continue through FY2026 and moderate thereafter. [CL-10, Guidance]

### Leadership, Governance and Three Lines Capacity

- Alanna Reyes became Group CEO in February 2026, with a mandate of simplification, cost discipline and completing the regulatory remediation agenda. Group Head of Compliance Martin Ashby is accountable for the financial crime remediation portfolio. COO Devan Mistry is accountable for platform modernisation. [CL-03, Executive Committee]
- The Group CRO post has been vacant since November 2025, with Deputy CRO Hannah Okafor providing interim cover. The Board agreed to an external search. Analysts see the absence of a permanent CRO as an execution risk. [CL-03, Executive Committee]
- Second line headcount is 214 against an establishment of 247, a gap of 33. Financial crime has the largest gap, with 19 unfilled roles. Peer data shows a 19% financial crime vacancy rate against a peer median of 11%. [CL-03, Second Line Structure]
- Second line is 4.5% of headcount against a peer median of 6.1%. Financial crime vacancy of 19% is in the highest-vacancy quartile. [EX-05, Peer Benchmarking]
- The FY2026 plan has 38 audits and needs about 6,200 days against 4,900 days of internal capacity. The shortfall of about 1,300 days is to be met by co-source arrangements, mainly in cyber, cloud, financial crime and resilience. [CL-09, Resourcing]
- The FY2025 effectiveness review found inconsistent risk reporting across regional entities, limited aggregation of third-party risk data, and no defined AI governance framework. [CL-07, Known Gaps]

### Strategy and Growth Plans

- The strategy targets a cost-to-income ratio of 62% by 2028 through automation, process standardisation and consolidation into two hubs. It also targets doubling fund administration and depositary revenue by 2028, focused on Luxembourg and Singapore. [CL-04, Strategic Ambition]
- Institutional Services growth is expected to increase onboarding volumes of complex corporate and fund structures by about 140%. [CL-04, Four Strategic Pillars]
- The strategy depends on three enablers: completing financial crime remediation, a group-wide operational resilience capability meeting supervisory expectations, and filling second line vacancies. Management flagged that failing to complete remediation would constrain regulatory approval for Luxembourg and Singapore expansion. [CL-04, Dependencies and Enablers]
- The Board raised the onboarding-volume appetite threshold in Institutional Services on the basis that financial crime controls had been remediated and assessed as effective. That basis is contested (see the remediation section). [CL-05, Item 7 — Risk Appetite Review]
- Firms pursuing growth in regulated activity before completing control remediation are materially more likely to face supervisory intervention. [EX-04, Observation on Sequencing]
- The Institutional Services growth ambition is sound in principle but concentrates the Group in the activity attracting the greatest supervisory scrutiny. Financial crime overhang is likely to constrain inorganic options and entity expansion. [EX-08, Implications for Meridian]

### Financial Crime Remediation Status (Contested)

- The Board was told the programme is 'substantially complete'. All twelve high-priority findings are said to be closed, with closure evidence accepted by second line. Three medium-priority findings remain open. The Group Head of Compliance confirmed no high-priority findings are open, and the Chair asked that the programme be prepared for formal closure. [CL-05, Item 6 — Financial Crime Remediation Programme]
- Per persons familiar with the supervisory dialogue, supervisors do not regard the programme as complete. Four high-priority findings are said to remain open, including beneficial ownership verification and sanctions screening tuning. Some findings reported as closed were reportedly reopened after independent validation found the evidence insufficient. This is UNRESOLVED: no document shows independent validation of Meridian's closures, and EX-07 rests on unnamed sources. [EX-07, Remediation Status Questioned]
- Investor messaging says remediation is 'well advanced and moving to completion'. The annual report says certain workstreams extend into the next financial year. Neither confirms or refutes either position above. [CL-10, Key Messages to Market]
- The client had considered independent validation of remediation closure but deferred it pending completion of the programme. This means no independent validated position currently exists in the corpus. [PV-09, Areas Discussed with the Client]
- A peer firm told its board a remediation programme was complete, but independent testing later found findings closed without adequate evidence. The supervisor called inaccurate board reporting the most serious aggravating factor and said boards must test closure evidence rather than rely on assertion. This is context, not evidence about Meridian. [EX-03, Regulatory Commentary]
- Meridian's sanctions screening tuning was last reviewed in 2023, and the related audit was deferred from FY2025 into FY2026. This is consistent with EX-07's naming of sanctions tuning as an open finding, but does not confirm it. [CL-08, Core Platforms]

### Operational Resilience, Third-Party and Technology

- Mapping of third-party dependencies for the four most critical services is incomplete. Only 40% of important business services have completed dependency mapping against a peer median of 78%. Supervisors treat incomplete mapping as a material shortcoming. [EX-05, Peer Benchmarking]
- Of three critical suppliers, only Supplier A (core hosting) has an exit plan. Supplier B (fund administration) and Supplier C (payments and screening) have none, and Supplier C's contract has no right-to-audit clause. [CL-08, Critical Third-Party Suppliers]
- The Board requested a third-party remediation plan by end of Q1 2026. Audit rights and incident notification provisions are inconsistent across suppliers. [CL-06, Item 5 — Third-Party Risk]
- A Q4 2025 third-party hosting failure disrupted client servicing for about 31 hours. The Group says no client data was compromised and the incident was reported to supervisors. [EX-07, Remediation Status Questioned]
- The core platform programme is about four months behind baseline, data platform wave one has slipped to Q2 2026, and forecast spend is up GBP 14m. Prior Protiviti assurance found optimistic milestone reporting. [CL-06, Item 4 — Core Platform Modernisation Programme]
- Privileged access reviews are incomplete in the Asian entities. Vulnerability SLAs were missed in each of the last four quarters. Logging and monitoring does not cover all business-critical applications. [CL-08, Known Technology Control Gaps]
- About 34% of workloads are cloud-hosted, against a peer median of 52%. Security baselines are not consistently applied across regional entities. [EX-05, Peer Benchmarking]

### AI Governance

- Management acknowledges there is no approved AI governance framework, AI inventory or generative-AI model risk controls. These are prerequisites to deployment beyond pilot scale. The AI governance forum has not been constituted, and no consolidated model inventory exists. [CL-04, Artificial Intelligence Ambition]
- CL-04 describes the third AI domain as 'internal audit and risk analytics' in one section and as 'internal knowledge search' in the pillar text. The corpus does not say which is current, so both are reported. [CL-04, Four Strategic Pillars]
- The AI governance readiness audit is deferred pending creation of the framework. Meridian scores 0% against a peer median of 45% on the approved-framework metric. [CL-09, Thematic Coverage]
- Supervisors expect an AI inventory, proportionate governance and human oversight, and indicate firms should not scale deployment ahead of governance. [EX-01, Priority Area Six — Artificial Intelligence]

### Conduct and Legacy Redress

- A GBP 31m legacy product governance provision has been recognised. The affected customer population is still under review and a further provision cannot be ruled out. [CL-06, Item 8 — Consumer Outcomes]
- Redress provisions are frequently revised upwards after initial recognition, and supervisors expect firms to identify affected populations in legacy products. [EX-04, Top Risk Themes]

### Regulatory and Sector Context

- A thematic review of 14 institutional services firms found inadequate beneficial ownership identification at nine, untuned sanctions screening at eight, and resourcing lagging onboarding growth at eleven. Closure by management assertion rather than independent validation was found in several cases. [EX-02, Principal Findings]
- Supervisors expect a complete register of material outsourcing, exit plans for all critical suppliers, contractual audit rights and a concentration assessment. [EX-01, Priority Area Three — Third-Party and Outsourcing Risk]
- Supervisors are moving toward demonstrable effectiveness testing and independent validation of remediation closure. [EX-09, Financial Crime]
- Firms in Meridian's position typically increase spend on external assurance and specialist advisory support during remediation and validation. [EX-08, View]

### Protiviti Relationship and Relevant Capability

- Protiviti was appointed in 2024 for a two-year internal audit co-source covering a subset of disciplines. Financial crime audit scope was retained in-house. In FY2024 it delivered 14 of 15 planned audits. [PV-01, Outcome]
- Protiviti was appointed for a 2025 cyber security assessment, with fieldwork in 2025 and reporting to the Technology and Operational Resilience Committee. [PV-02, Outcome]
- Engagement was strongest with the Chief Internal Auditor and CTO. Access to the second line financial crime team was limited. Client resource availability disrupted scheduling twice. [PV-05, [PV-05, Relationship Observations]]
- Protiviti tested closure evidence for 41 findings at a European bank, found 9 closed without sufficient evidence, and reported a validated position to the board and supervisor. [PV-03, [PV-03, Credential 1 — Financial Crime Remediation Validation, European Bank]]
- Protiviti's methodology treats closure validation as a distinct assurance activity. A finding counts as closed only where the control is demonstrated to operate effectively. [PV-04, [PV-04, Closure Validation Principle]]
- Additional scope would be contracted by separate statement of work under the existing MSA. The client had not confirmed whether the FY2026 co-source would be extended, expanded to financial crime, or re-tendered. [PV-09, Open Items at Time of Writing]

### Recommended Engagement Focus Areas

- Make establishing Meridian's actual remediation position the engagement's first priority. The board record and the press report conflict and cannot be resolved from the corpus. The risk appetite increase and expansion plans depend on the board version. Protiviti's closure validation credential and methodology address this directly. Raise it carefully, since the client deferred validation pending programme completion. [PV-03, [PV-03, Credential 1 — Financial Crime Remediation Validation, European Bank]]
- Prepare the team to discuss board-reporting quality and the distinction between management assertion and independently validated position. The peer enforcement case cited inaccurate board reporting as the most serious aggravating factor, and the press report draws the parallel to Meridian. [EX-03, Regulatory Commentary]
- Focus on whether control capacity is being scaled ahead of the roughly 140% onboarding growth. The board raised the onboarding appetite, financial crime has 19 unfilled roles, and supervisors expect capacity to be scaled in advance. A control capacity plan with volume triggers is an engagement opening. [CL-03, Second Line Structure]
- Pursue the deferred sanctions screening tuning audit as a concrete follow-on. Tuning was last reviewed in 2023, the audit was carried forward from FY2025, and Protiviti has a matching credential. EX-07 also names tuning as an open finding. [PV-05, [PV-05, Deferred Audit]]
- Position third-party exit planning and dependency mapping as a priority. Two of three critical suppliers lack exit plans, only 40% of services have completed mapping, and Supplier C has no audit right. Exit planning was scoped but not commissioned, and the client intends to do mapping internally first, so engage by offering assurance or acceleration. [CL-08, Critical Third-Party Suppliers]
- Engage on the FY2026 co-source decision. The plan has a shortfall of about 1,300 audit days, the Audit Committee has asked for a review of co-source sustainability, and the client has not decided whether to extend, expand to financial crime, or re-tender. [CL-09, Resourcing]
- Offer AI governance framework design before any scaled AI deployment. Meridian has no framework, inventory or forum, and supervisors say governance must precede scale. The CTO discussion was only exploratory. Clarify the intended AI scope first, since CL-04 is internally inconsistent. [CL-07, Governance Structure Beneath the Committee]
- Consider independent programme assurance over the platform modernisation. The programme is four months late with GBP 14m of extra forecast spend, the 62% cost-to-income target depends on it, and Protiviti's earlier work found optimistic milestone reporting. [PV-05, [PV-05, Principal Themes Reported]]
- Explore redress population identification and assurance. The population is undetermined, a further provision is possible, and Protiviti's catalogue includes this service. [CL-10, Analyst Question and Answer Summary]

## 2. Conflicts and Reconciliation

**Subject:** Status of the financial crime remediation programme: how many findings remain open, and at what priority

- **Source A:** The Board was told the programme is 'substantially complete'. All twelve high-priority findings from the 2024 supervisory review are remediated and closed. Three medium-priority findings remain open, due to close within the quarter. The Group Head of Compliance confirmed that no high-priority findings are open. [CL-05, Item 6 — Financial Crime Remediation Programme]
- **Source B:** Citing persons familiar with the supervisory dialogue, the press report says supervisors do not regard the programme as complete. It says four high-priority findings remain open, including beneficial ownership verification and sanctions screening tuning. It also says some findings reported as closed were reopened after independent validation testing found the closure evidence insufficient. [EX-07, Remediation Status Questioned]
- **Resolution basis:** unresolved
- **Resolution:** Neither source can be preferred on the evidence available. CL-05 is a primary record, but it reflects management's own assertion, and no document shows independent validation of the closures. EX-07 is secondary and relies on unnamed sources, and it does not reproduce the supervisory correspondence (EX-07 coverage gap candidate). CL-06's confidential annex on supervisory correspondence is also not available. EX-02, EX-03, EX-09 and PV-04 show that closure by management assertion is often unreliable, but that is context and not evidence about Meridian. CL-10 ('well advanced and moving to completion') and CL-01 (some workstreams extend into next year) are softer than CL-05 and neither confirms nor refutes either position. PV-09 says independent validation was deferred. The dispute should be flagged as open and should not be resolved by recency or authority.

**Subject:** Third domain of the planned AI deployment: internal audit and risk analytics versus internal knowledge search

- **Source A:** The Group intends to deploy AI across onboarding document triage, adviser knowledge assistance, and internal audit and risk analytics. [CL-04, Artificial Intelligence Ambition]
- **Source B:** Pillar four lists the AI-assisted domains as client onboarding triage, adviser support, and internal knowledge search, subject to establishment of a group AI governance framework. [CL-04, Four Strategic Pillars]
- **Resolution basis:** unresolved
- **Resolution:** Both statements are in the same document, with no date or hierarchy to separate them. The difference may be wording or may be a real change of scope. Nothing in the corpus says which version is current, so the pack should report both and not choose one.
